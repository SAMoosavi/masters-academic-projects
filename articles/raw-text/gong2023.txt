ABSTRACT
Certificateless aggregate signature (CLAS) is an effective approach for the multiple authentications in multiusers and multi-information environments like the vehicular ad hoc networks (VANETs), which can address
the certificate management issue in the traditional public key cryptography (PKC) and solve the key escrow
problem in identity-based cryptography (IBC). In recent years, a new CLAS mechanism (LICLAS) is presented for
the authentication of sensors in healthcare wireless medical sensor networks (HWMSNs). In this paper, we make
an analysis and prove that LICLAS is not able to against forgery attacks. Thus, we present an improved CLAS
scheme (PCAS) for vehicle authentication in VANETs, which satisfies the privacy and security requirements.
PCAS constructs the signature algorithm based on Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECC) to avoid bilinear pairing
and Map-to-Hash function operations. In order to further improve security and efficiency of PCAS, the vehicle
key generation and signature algorithm is revised. The pseudonym mechanism is also adopted to achieve
conditional privacy preservation. Besides, PCAS allows to execute a batch verification of messages, where
RSUs may verify the aggregated signatures from vehicles to reduce time overhead. Under the assumption that
the elliptic curve discrete logarithm problem (ECDLP) is hard, PCAS is proved to be existentially unforgeable
against adaptive chosen message attacks in the random oracle model. Through the performance analysis, under
the same time complexity, PCAS is shown to more effective. Compared with LICLAS, for a single message
and 2000 messages, the transmission overhead of PCAS is reduced by 25% and 25% respectively, and the
computation overhead is reduced by 16.56% and 25.34% respectively.

1. Introduction
The appearance of vehicular ad-hoc networks (VANETs) is to meet
the requirements of the development of wireless communication technology and vehicle communication [1]. VANETs is a decentralized,
ad-hoc network that supports multi-hop forwarding, which can be regarded as an advancement of Manet [2]. It can prevent traffic accidents,
reduce traffic jams, provide convenient services, improve information communication, enhance the driving experiences, and provide
safety-related services such as curve speed warning, pedestrian crossing warning, collision warning, etc. In VANETs, the on board unit
(OBU) equipped on the vehicle can act as a message processor [3],
which can collect, process, and send traffic-related messages to provide information support for the vehicle. Vehicles are free to keep
connected to the network without relying on a fixed infrastructure.
To provide communication services for vehicles, the road side units
(RSUs) are deployed to the roadside. There are two categories of vehicle
communication: vehicle-to-vehicle (V2V), and vehicle-to-infrastructure

(V2I). The V2I communication is based on secure channels, while the
V2V communication uses the Dedicated Short Range Communication
(DSRC) protocol [4]. According to the DSRC protocol, the vehicle needs
to broadcast traffic-related information (such as driving conditions,
direction, speed, road congestion, etc.) around the vehicle every 100–
300 ms. Besides, VANETs data communication speed is between 6 and
27 Mbps, covering a range of 1000 m. By collecting and processing the
received traffic-related messages, emergency services can be provided
for vehicles [5]. For example, in the event of a road traffic accident,
drivers can take corresponding actions or change the driving route of
the vehicle.
Due to the communication in an open-access environment, VANETs
is vulnerable to a variety of security attacks [6–9]. Common attacks
include sniffing, modification, replay, inject, impersonation attack, etc.
These attacks pose a serious threat to the life and property of drivers.
Since the attacker can receive traffic messages, if it modifies or deletes
relevant safety messages, traffic jams and accidents may occur, thus

To solve these problems, the digital signature is used to the authenticity of messages and the pseudonym mechanism is used to achieve
conditional privacy preservation [10]. In recent years, many authentication schemes have been proposed. These schemes can be roughly divided into four categories: public key cryptography-based (PKC), group
signature and ring signature-based, identity-based, and certificateless
public key cryptography-based (CL-PKC).
In a PKC-based scheme, the certificate authority (CA) is responsible
for issuing certificates to users [11]. The existence of the certificate
proves the validity of the user’s public key and pseudonym. However, the PKC-based schemes suffer from the certificate management
problem. The life cycle of a certificate is the generation, issuance,
verification and revocation. The process of verifying a certificate is
complex and time-consuming. A large number of certificates will bring
huge storage and computation overhead. The group signature and ring
signature-based schemes are proposed to enhance the anonymity of
users. However, due to the need to manage a large number of group
members, these schemes have a huge computation overhead [12,13].
To solve the certificate management issue, Shamir [14] presented
an Identity-Based Signature in 1984. The public key can be calculated
based on the owner’s identity, such as the email address, phone number, IP number, etc. The Private Key Generator (PKG) is responsible for
generating the private keys. Since the PKG manages the private keys
of all entities, the identity-based schemes suffer from the key escrow
problem. The concept of key escrow is the practice of holding the
keys for decrypting encrypted data in escrow so that, under certain
circumstances, an authorized third party may gain access to them. A
key escrow problem occurs when a third party can access the key, as
the third party can use the key to decrypt confidential information.
To solve the key escrow problem, certificateless public key cryptography (CL-PKC) is proposed by Al-Riyami et al. [15] in 2003. In
a CL-PKC scheme, the key generation center (KGC) is responsible
for generating the partial secret key of entities. Then, an entity can
generate its private key by combining the partial secret key and the
secret value selected by itself. When an attacker only knows the partial
secret key of the entity, it cannot generate the entity’s private key.
Thus, the key escrow problem is resolved. Then, in order to improve
the efficiency of verification, an aggregate signature is first proposed
in [16]. This scheme can map n signatures on n different messages to a
signature. After that, many certificateless aggregate signature (CLAS)
schemes have been proposed. The CLAS schemes solve not only the
certificate management issue, but also the key escrow problem, as well
as enhanced the verification efficiency. Recently, a pairing-free certificateless aggregate signature approach for the wireless medical sensor
networks (HWMSNs) was presented by Gayathri et al. [17]. However,
the research of Liu et al. [18] demonstrated that the scheme [17] is
insecure. An attack model was proposed in [18] to prove that the
scheme [17] could not resist the forgery attack under the two types
of adversaries. To improve security, the scheme [18] used a hash
function to solve the problems in [17]. Compared with the recent
papers, the scheme [18] has better performance in computation and
communication overhead. Unfortunately, through our research, the
scheme [18] still cannot resist the forgery signature attack. Then, we
improved the signature algorithm and propose a new certificateless
aggregate signature scheme (PCAS) for VANETs.

2. Related works
A large number of authentication schemes based on traditional
public key cryptography (PKC) have been applied to VANETs to achieve
privacy-preserving [19,20]. In [19], a anonymous authentication
scheme for VANETs was proposed. In this scheme, the authors use
short-term pseudonyms to achieve hierarchical aggregation. Azees et al.
[20] put forward an authentication scheme for VANETs by using
bilinear pairing to achieve conditional privacy-preserving. In [20],
without TA, the vehicles and RSUs can authenticate each other. The
storage and management of a large number of certificates will bring a
huge computation and storage overhead, which will greatly reduce the
efficiency of the schemes.
In group signature and ring signature-based schemes, the group
is composed of a manager and members. Each group issues a public
key and a individual private key to each member. It is impossible for
an adversary to link two signatures produced by the same vehicle,
thus achieving unlinkability. In [12], the authors proposed a threshold anonymous authentication scheme based on group signature. The
new scheme in [12] reduces the heavy burden of group certificate
generation from TA. However, their scheme consists of many complex operations such as the creation and maintenance of a group.
To achieve privacy-preserving, a group signature based authentication
algorithm has been presented in [13]. Their scheme supports vehicle
revocation and uses batch verification to improve efficiency. In [21],
the authors proposed a lattice-based double authentication preventing
ring signature scheme. Different from other schemes, there is no group
administrator in their scheme, and each group member has equal status.
In 2020, Mundhe et al. [22] designed a ring signature scheme using lattice to achieve unconditional and location privacy. Although the group
signature and ring signature-based schemes have good performance,
the management and maintenance of a large number of members
increase the complexity of the system.
To solve the certificate issue in PKC-based schemes, identity-based
cryptography schemes are widely used [23–26]. In [23], the authors
use identity-based batch verification to improve authentication efficiency. Due to the complexity of anonymous identity generation,
signing, and verification, the scheme in [23] has poor performance.
In [24], the authors presented an identity-based aggregate signature
scheme with a designated verifier to keep data integrity. To achieve
the privacy-preserving and support the revocation of vehicles, Wang
et al. [25] designed an identity-based authentication scheme. In [25],

3.3. Security requirements of VANETs
Based on previous works in VANETs, our scheme should meet the
following security and privacy requirements:
• Message Authentication, Integrity and non-repudiation: To ensure
the security of user communication, the verifier must confirm that
the message is from a legitimate user, non-modified, and cannot
be denied.
• Identity privacy-preserving: To protect users’ real identity and
other private information, no one can extract the sender’s real
identity from the signed message, except TA.
• unlinkability: No one could link any two signatures to the same
signer, except TA.
• Traceability: If there are malicious vehicles on the road sending
false information to cause traffic chaos or accidents, the TA must
be able to extract the real identity of such vehicles to achieve
revocation.
• Attack resistance: The proposed scheme for VANETs should be
able to withstand various attacks, such as replay, impersonation,
modification, Man-in-the-middle, Malicious KGC, and so on.

As shown in Fig. 2, we present a traditional two-layer system model
in VANETs. The lower layer contains two kinds of communication (V2V
and V2I). Both kinds of communication are based on the DSRC [4]
protocol. The upper layer is composed of TA, KGC, and AS. They
communicate with each other through a secure transmission protocol.
1. TA is a Trusted Authority tasked with the initialization of systems. Both RSUs and vehicles need to register with TA before entering the VANETs. TA can track malicious vehicles and revoke
them by extracting their real identity from their pseudonyms.
2. KGC is a trusted third party, which generates the partial secret
key for the vehicle.
3. AS is responsible for collecting traffic-related information from
RSUs. By analyzing the information, it can provide corresponding services (e.g., entertainment and advertisement recommendations) for the entities in VANETs. Besides, AS can support RSU
by verifying the aggregate signature to reduce verification time.
4. RSU is an essential device installed on the roadside, which
can provide communication services for vehicles within communication range. It can independently verify the signatures of
vehicles, and also generate aggregate signatures by collecting the
signatures of multiple vehicles.
5. OBU is installed in the vehicle which can broadcast trafficrelated information using the DSRC protocol.

7. Discussion

Declaration of competing interest

Aiming at the improvement of Liu et.’s scheme, this paper presents
a new certificateless aggregate signature scheme for VANETs (PCAS).
PCAS satisfies all the privacy and security requirements for VANETs.
However, there are several open problems that need to solve in the
future.

The authors declare that they have no known competing financial interests or personal relationships that could have appeared to
influence the work reported in this paper.
Data availability

• Pseudonym. A limitation of our study is that the pseudonym
generation process is too complicated, and there are too many
entity interactions, which brings a large part of the computation overhead. Therefore, it is necessary to propose an efficient
pseudonym generation scheme. In [37], the authors designed a
pseudonym generation algorithm, which supports vehicles to calculate pseudonyms by themselves. The design of the pseudonym
algorithm is one aspect of our future work. In addition, to protect
the full location privacy and security of communication, the
vehicle needs to constantly change the pseudonym during driving.
However, much of the current literature on pseudonym change
pays attention to the application layer, while the MAC layer is
not considered. If the MAC address is fixed, the adversaries could
still obtain the location privacy of the tracked vehicle.
• Bath verification. Under a high-density traffic environment in
VANETs, many safety messages need to be verified in time. The
emergence of the aggregate signature scheme realizes batch verification. When the verification of the aggregate signature fails, it
needs to be re-verified, which brings huge computation overhead.
Therefore, it is necessary to improve the process of aggregate
verification. Moreover, past research focused on the design of
signatures rather than how to batch verification can be improved.
Furthermore, many schemes were designed based on fixed batch
sizes instead of dynamic batch sizes. Due to the dynamic nature
of VANETs, the dynamic batch size can be used to improve the
efficiency of batch verification. Another issue is how to reduce
the number of expired and unverified BSMs when a large number
of BSMs need to be verified in time. In the future, we will continue
to study how to improve the efficiency of batch verification,
while ensuring that the scheme meets the security and privacy
requirements of VANETs.

8. Conclusion
In this paper, we prove that the certificateless aggregate signature
scheme in HWMSNs proposed by Liu et al. [18] is insecure against a
forgery attack. Aimed to improve security and decrease energy consumption, we present a new CLAS scheme PCAS that uses Elliptic Curve
Cryptography and avoids bilinear pairing and Map-to-point Hash function. We redesign the vehicle key generation and signature algorithm
with pairing-free to improve security and performance. Besides, the
pseudonym mechanism is used in PCAS to achieve conditional privacy
preservation. PCAS allows RSU to aggregate the signatures sent from
vehicles and send the aggregate signature to AS for verification, which
realizes batch verification of messages. Under the assumption that the
elliptic curve discrete logarithm problem is hard, PCAS is existentially
unforgeable against adaptive chosen message attacks in the random
oracle model. In addition, we demonstrate that PCAS satisfies all the
privacy and security requirements for VANETs, and is secure against
replay, impersonation, malicious KGC, and other security attacks. Theoretical analysis and simulation show that PCAS outperforms several
existing schemes in computation and transmission overhead with the
same time complexity. Compared with the scheme [18], for a single
message and 2000 messages, the communication overhead of PCAS
is reduced by 25% and 25% respectively, and the computation overhead is reduced by 16.56% and 25.34% respectively. The superior
performance of PCAS makes it suitable for vehicle authentication in
VANETs.
