A B S T R A C T
Vehicular Ad Hoc Networks (VANETs) are high-speed mobile wireless communication networks that play a
pivotal role in shaping the future of intelligent transportation systems. In many fields, certificateless aggregate
signcryption (CLASC) serves as a reliable method that reduces computational and communication overheads while
enhancing security. Due to these advantages, in recent years, an increasing number of signcryption schemes have
been presented in VANETs. In this paper, a security analysis of Dai et al.’s pairing-free CLASC scheme for vehicular
sensor networks is conducted. The analysis results reveal its vulnerability to public key replacement attacks. To
address this security flaw, a security-enhanced certificateless aggregate signcryption scheme is proposed and
demonstrated its co­fidentiality and unforgeability against adaptive chosen ciphertext/message attack in the
random oracle model. Additionally, the proposed scheme is demonstrated to satisfy the security attributes in
VANETs. Eventually, the performance analysis reveal that the proposed CLASC scheme demonstrates superior
computational and communication eﬃciency compared to the related schemes.

1. Introduction
VANETs is an application of IoT in transportation designed to en­
able communication between vehicles (V2V) and between vehicles and
roadside infrastructure (V2I). These networks adopt some advanced IoT
technologies such as sensor data acquisition and Dedicated Short Range
Communications (DSRC) protocol [1] to achieve mutual communication
and real-time traﬃc information exchange between V2I.
In VANETs, vehicles act as mobile nodes with onboard units (OBUs)
containing various sensing devices such as GPS, cameras, and distance
sensors. These OBUs collect data on driving parameters, road condi­
tions, and traﬃc patterns. According to the DSRC protocol, vehicles
are required to periodically broadcast tra˙ic-related information at in­
tervals of 100--300 milliseconds [2]. Roadside units (RSUs) act as a
bridge that relays information between vehicles and the upper layer of
the network, which typically consisting of key generation center (KGC)
and application servers (AS) managed by the traﬃc administrations.
RSUs collect these information and forward it to AS, which provides
various services to other vehicles in VANETs based on the received
information.
The dynamic nature of VANETs, characterized by rapidly moving
nodes and frequently changing network topologies, presents unique

challenges in terms of network management, security, and privacy [3].
For example, a range of potential security risks such as impersonation,
eavesdropping, data mod­fication, and tracking pose signiﬁcant threats
to the integrity and co­fidentiality of data exchanged within VANETs.
Therefore, addressing the security and privacy issues in VANETs has
been brought to the fore.
In VANETs, three critical issues require focus: firstly, ensuring ef­
ﬁcient authentication of the source’s reliability; secondly, protecting
vehicle information to maintain privacy; and lastly, enabling traceabil­
ity of the identities of malicious vehicles.
Therefore, information shared in VANETs must adhere to secu­
rity requirements such as authentication, integrity, co­fidentiality,
anonymity, traceability and revocation.
To meet these security requirements, researchers have proposed
many provable authentication schemes, where digital signature and
pseudonym techniques have been employed to achieve authentication
and privacy protection, respectively. Many early schemes [4,5] relied
on the traditional Public Key Infrastructure (PKI) system, necessitat­
ing the certificate authority to manage certificates for users. This im­
poses signiﬁcant burdens in storage and computation overhead. To solve
the limitations of traditional PKI authentication schemes, numerous
identity-based public key cryptography (ID-PKC) schemes [6,7] have

challenges, some researchers have embraced certificateless public key
cryptography (CL-PKC), in which KGC generates a portion of the user’s
private key, enabling the user to generate the other remaining portion

signature schemes [8,9] were proposed, eﬀectively eliminating certifi­
cate management and key escrow problems.
Nevertheless, the majority of the certificateless signature schemes
do not adequately address the co­fidentiality of transferred data, which
may result in the exposure of user information. To address this is­
sue, Zheng et al. [10] proposed a cryptographic primitive called sign­
cryption, which combines signature and encryption into a single step,
thereby oﬀering greater eﬃciency. Its primary advantage lies in reduc­
ing computation and communication overheads while enhancing secu­
rity. Furthermore, to enhance the eﬃciency of signature ver­fication,
the concept of aggregate signatures was introduced [11]. Subsequently,
the combination of signcryption and aggregate signature techniques has
led to the emergence of numerous certificateless aggregate signcryption
schemes (CLASC), as listed in Table 1. However, it is worth noting that
these schemes exhibit certain shortcomings. They either fail to meet se­
curity requirements or demonstrate low communication eﬃciency due
to their reliance on bilinear pairings to construct CLASC schemes.
Recently, Dai et al. [12] proposed a CLASC scheme based on elliptic
curve cryptography (ECC), claiming that it provides co­fidentiality and
authentication for VANETs applications. However, our research reveals
that their scheme exists security vulnerability, making it susceptible to
Type-I adversary that can forge signcryption in their security model.
This motivates us to enhance Dai et al.’s scheme by improving its secu­
rity while ensuring eﬃciency. Additionally, to make it better suitable
for VANETs applications, our proposed scheme includes mechanisms to
promptly trace and revoke malicious vehicles after an attack.

3. The computation and communication overheads of the proposed

2. Related work
In 2003, Boneh et al. [14] introduced a novel cryptographic prim­
itive called aggregate signatures, which garnered signiﬁcant attention
in the field of cryptography. In 2008, Barbosa et al. [15] proposed the
ﬁrst certificateless signcryption (CLSC) scheme. Later, in 2011, Lu and
Xie [16] introduced CLASC scheme within the framework of CL-PKC.
Since then, numerous CLASC schemes have been developed, which can
be broadly categorized into two types based on their construction meth­
ods: bilinear pairing-based schemes and pairing-free schemes.
For bilinear pairing-based CLASC schemes, in 2016, Yin et al. [17]
proposed a new provably secure CLASC scheme using bilinear pair­
ings. In the same year, Chen and Ren [18] designed a CLASC-based
privacy-preserving scheme for smart grids, although it failed to meet
data co­fidentiality requirements. In 2020, Kim et al. [19] proposed
a pairing-based CLASC scheme for the Internet of Things (IoT), which
addressed the key escrow problem and proved to be eﬃcient for IoT
infrastructure. In 2022, Yang et al. [21] developed a blockchain-based
eﬃcient CLASC scheme for IoT maritime system. Similarly, Zhang et al.
[22] introduced a privacy-preserving CLASC scheme for safety warning
system in fog-cloud based VANET, and Dohare et al. [27] proposed a
CLASC scheme for cloud fog concentric industry 4.0, claiming to pro­
vide co­fidentiality and authentication for industrial IoT applications.
In 2023, Xu et al. [23] presented a blockchain-based certificateless sign­
cryption mechanism for edge computing, but their scheme was found
to be vulnerable to Type-I adversary signature forgery attacks. How­
ever, most bilinear pairing-based CLASC schemes are unsuitable for
resource-constrained VANETs [24] due to their time-consuming pair­
ing operations.
For pairing-free CLASC schemes, in 2018, Ullah et al. [25] proposed
an ECC-based CLASC scheme to protect smart cameras. However, Kim
et al. [19] pointed out that Ullah’s scheme was susceptible to key theft
issues and failed to ensure the claimed co­fidentiality and authenti­

3.2. System model
As illustrated in Fig. 1, the descriptions of the relevant entities are
provided below.
KGC: It is a fully trusted authority with suﬃcient computing and
communication resources. It generates some system-shared parameters.
It is also responsible for revocation key managements, tracking, and re­
vocation of malicious vehicles.
AS: It ver­fies the messages from RSU to get information about traﬃc
situations and optimize traﬃc light control.
RSU: RSU is deployed at the roadside and acts as a bridge between
AS and Vehicle. RSU is wired to AS and KGC, and wireless to vehicles. It
is tasked with aggregating the signcrypted messages from vehicles and
sending the result to AS. Vehicle: every vehicle is equipped with onboard
units (OBUs) containing a variety of devices such as GPS, cameras and
distance sensors [36], and it broadcasts tra˙ic-related information and
driving status to improve the road environment.
