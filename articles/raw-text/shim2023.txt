ABSTRACT In order to solve security and privacy-preservation issues in VANETs, a number of conditional
privacy-preservation schemes based on certificateless signature (CLS) schemes and certificateless aggregate
signature (CLAS) schemes have been proposed. However, most of them were insecure against several types
of attacks. Recently, Wang et al. and Xiong et al. proposed new conditional privacy-preserving authentication
schemes based on CLAS and CLS schemes, respectively, for VANETs. We present malicious-but-passive
KGC attacks on Wang et al.’ scheme, and forgery attacks and key recovery attacks on Xiong et al.’ scheme,
where KGC or anyone can generate valid signatures on any messages for any vehicles without being
traced by TA. Our attacks break their unforgeability and traceability. At last, we discuss their causes and
countermeasures.
INDEX TERMS Certificateless signature scheme, certificateless aggregate signature, forgery attack, key
recovery attack, malicious-but-passive KGC attack.

I. INTRODUCTION

ITS and VANET technologies play an important role in
real-time traffic condition monitoring, prevention of collisions, safety, blind crossing, dynamic route scheduling and
provide users with various improved services [1], [2], [3].
However, security and privacy-preservation in VANETs are
still challenging issues.
Al-Riyami and Paterson [4] proposed certificateless
public-key cryptography to resolve the certificate management problem of public-key cryptography [5] and the
key-escrow problem of identity (ID)-based cryptography [6].
In certificateless public-key cryptography, a user private key
is computed by combining a partial private key generated
by Key Generate Center (KGC) with a user-chosen secret.
Certificateless public-key cryptography does not require
public-key certificates and ciphertext/signatures are transmitted with users’ public keys. With these advantages, certificateless signature (CLS) schemes seem to be suitable
for achieving authenticity and integrity of transmitted data

III. CRYPTANALYSIS OF TWO SCHEMES

There are two kinds of secrets in the CLS and CLAS schemes:
one is the master secret of the KGC and the secret key of each
user. Obviously, if the two kinds of secrets are compromised
then the schemes are entirely broken, but if one of the secret
keys is compromised then the scheme must be secure. Based
on the kinds of the secret keys, the attacks on the scheme
can be divided into two types attacks, a type I attack and a
type II attack. Security of the type I attack means that the
scheme is unforgeable under the compromise of the secret
key of a target user, while that of the type II attack means
that it is unforgeable under the compromise of the master
secret of the KGC. A malicious-but-passive KGC attacks as
a special case of the type II attacks, where the KGC could
control the system parameters dishonestly to forge users’
valid signatures. The two types of adversaries are defined in
terms of their capabilities:
• A type I adversary is a malicious third party who can
replace a user public key
• A type II adversary is a malicious third party who can
replace a vehicle public key and a type II adversary is a
malicious KGC who knows the master secret, but cannot
replace a user public key. Additionally, a malicious-butpassive-KGC is a kind of the type II adversary who can
generate the system parameters maliciously.
Details on the two formal adversarial models and system
model are defined in [25], [26].
The CLS and CLAS schemes are still digital signature
schemes. Thus, general forgery attacks on the digital signature schemes are possible, where an attacker has no information on the secret keys. Adversarial models of our forgery
attacks and key recovery attacks on Xiong et al.’s scheme are
defined as follows:
• Adversary’s capability: an adversary can obtain authentication messages in VANETs: it is plausible since
every authentication messages in VANETs are broadcast, so anyone can get them.
• Adversary’s goal: an adversary’s goal is to generate valid
authentication messages in VANET which cannot be
traced correctly by TA.
Now, we present malicious-but-passive-KGC attacks on
Wang et al.’s scheme, and forgery attacks and key recovery
attacks on Xiong et al.’s scheme.

IV. CAUSES AND COUNTERMEASURES

We discuss causes and countermeasures of the two schemes
against our attacks.
A. THE MALICIOUS-BUT-PASSIVE KGC ATTACKS ON
WANG ET AL.’S SCHEME

The malicious-but-passive KGC attacks illustrate how the
malicious KGC manipulates the system parameters to generate users’ valid signatures on any messages. Vulnerability

V. CONCLUSION

Recently, Wang et al. and Xiong et al. proposed new conditional privacy-preserving authentication schemes based on
CLS and CLAS schemes, respectively, for VANETs. We first
showed that Wang et al.’s scheme was insecure against the
malicious-but-passive attacks. We then show that, in Xiong
et al.’s scheme, anyone could recover the private keys of any
vehicles from only one signature and forged valid signatures
on any messages from two signatures without being traced by
TA. At last, we discussed their causes and countermeasures.
