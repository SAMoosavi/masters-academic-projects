ABSTRACT
Certificateless aggregate signature (CLAS) schemes enable multiple vehicles to combine their individual
signatures into a single compact authenticator, significantly reducing transmission and verification overhead in
bandwidth-constrained vehicular ad-hoc networks (VANETs). This efficiency makes CLAS particularly attractive
for real-time authentication in large-scale intelligent transportation systems (ITS). However, we show that two
representative CLAS-based conditional privacy-preserving authentication schemes, proposed by Zhu–Guan and
Chen–Guan, are vulnerable to a Type I adversary capable of replacing a vehicle’s public key. Such an adversary
can forge valid signatures on arbitrary messages without obtaining the vehicle’s partial private key due to
the lack of cryptographic binding between public keys and ephemeral randomness, which allows algebraic
elimination of the master public key during verification. We analyze the root causes of this flaw and propose
hash-based countermeasures that enforce strong binding among all public components. Our results further
reveal that conventional linear hash-dependent CLAS constructions face inherent limitations in balancing
security, ultra-low latency, and scalability. Thus, a next-generation CLAS design paradigm is required one that
moves beyond traditional linear architectures to achieve deployment-level performance in current 5G-V2X
environments while being inherently scalable toward future 6G-enabled intelligent transportation systems.

1. Introduction
Vehicular Ad-hoc Networks (VANETs) have emerged as a fundamental communication infrastructure for intelligent transportation systems (ITS), enabling real-time vehicle-to-vehicle (V2V) and vehicleto-infrastructure (V2I) communications to improve road safety, traffic
efficiency, and user experience [1–3]. In practical deployments such as
5G-V2X and ETSI ITS-G5, authentication must be completed within a
sub-10 ms latency budget to support the timely dissemination of safetycritical messages. However, due to their open wireless medium, high
mobility, and large-scale deployment, VANETs are inherently exposed
to various security and privacy threats, including message fabrication,
impersonation, and vehicle tracking. To address these challenges, a
variety of authentication mechanisms have been proposed based on
public-key infrastructure (PKI), identity-based cryptography (IBC), and
certificateless public key cryptography (CL-PKC). Among them, CLPKC [4] has received considerable attention as it eliminates certificate
management overhead in PKI while avoiding the key escrow problem
inherent in IBC.
From a cryptographic perspective, CL-PKC combines the advantages
of PKI and IBC by decoupling key generation between a trusted authority and users. Specifically, the trusted authority issues each user

a partial private key, while the user independently selects a secret
value to derive the full private key and the corresponding public key.
This key generation mechanism gives rise to two standard adversary
models. A Type I adversary is allowed to replace users’ public keys
but does not have access to the trusted authority’s master secret key,
whereas a Type II adversary knows the master secret key but cannot
perform public-key replacement. The security of certificateless signature schemes is commonly defined as existential unforgeability against
adaptive chosen-message attacks under these adversary models.
Aggregate signature techniques further enhance scalability by compressing multiple individual signatures into a single compact signature that can be verified efficiently. Certificateless aggregate signature
(CLAS) schemes integrate aggregate signatures with CL-PKC, thereby
reducing communication overhead and enabling efficient batch verification in large-scale VANET environments. Since vehicles typically
broadcast messages without prior trust relationships, security against
public-key replacement attacks by Type I adversaries is particularly
critical for CLAS-based VANET authentication schemes. Accordingly,
several CLAS-based conditional privacy-preserving authentication
(CPP) schemes have been proposed for VANET security [5–11]. In

3.3. Structural causes and countermeasures
We adopt the formal security model for CLAS schemes, considering
both Type I and Type II adversaries. In this model, a vehicle’s signing
capability is determined by a TA-issued partial private key and a userchosen secret value. A Type I adversary does not know the TA master
secret key but is allowed to arbitrarily replace public keys and request
signatures under the replaced keys, while a Type II adversary knows
the master secret key but cannot replace public keys. The security of
the proposed analysis is defined with respect to existential unforgeability against adaptive chosen-message attacks (EUF-CMA) under these
adversary models. The signing oracle queries made after public-key
replacement are explicitly allowed under the standard Type I adversary
model. In particular, 𝐼 may replace a target vehicle’s public key with
an adversary-chosen one and then obtain valid signatures with respect
to the replaced key, as long as the TA master secret is not revealed.
For detailed formal definitions and security games, we refer the reader
to [12,13]. The authors claim that their CLAS scheme is secure against
Type I and Type II adversaries in the random oracle model under the
hardness of the ECDLP (Elliptic Curev Discrete Logarithm Problem),
where the hash function is modeled as a random oracle. However, our
attacks demonstrate that their security proofs are flawed and that the
claimed security guarantee does not hold.
The vulnerabilities in both the Zhu-Guan and Chen-Guan CLAS
schemes stem from two fundamental structural causes. (i) The verification equations are constructed as linear combinations of independently
chosen elements, including public keys, ephemeral randomness, and
hash outputs. This structural linearity allows a Type I adversary, under
public-key replacement, to algebraically manipulate the ephemeral
component 𝐵 so as to cancel the TA’s master public key term ℎ3 ⋅ 𝑃pub ,
thereby bypassing the intended trust anchoring without knowledge of
the master secret 𝑠. (ii) The schemes fail to tightly bind extracted
signing material to the message context. Through a signing oracle
query, an adversary can extract reusable secret information without
knowing either the TA’s master secret or the victim’s partial private
key. In particular, in the Chen-Guan scheme, the extracted value 𝜃 is not
cryptographically bound to the message or timestamp, which enables
its reuse to forge valid signatures on different messages.
These two structural flaws, namely linear verifiability and insufficient message binding, not only undermine formal security guarantees
but also pose serious risks in practical VANET deployments. An attacker
exploiting these weaknesses could inject forged safety messages in
real time, threatening the reliability and safety of large-scale vehicular
networks.

Practical Limitation. While this modification successfully restores
security, it increases aggregate verification overhead to approximately
3𝑛 + 2 scalar multiplications. This cost is non-trivial for high-density
VANET scenarios and highlights that heavy hash-dependent linear
constructions are fundamentally inefficient for real-time deployment.
These results make it clear that CLAS schemes must evolve beyond linear hash-binding architectures. A next-generation CLAS design
paradigm is required–one that inherently prevents algebraic manipulation while achieving strong security, ultra-low latency, and massive
scalability for practical 5G-V2X and future 6G-enabled ITS deployments.
4. Conclusion
We have demonstrated that the Zhu–Guan and Chen–Guan CLASbased conditional privacy-preserving authentication schemes for
VANETs are vulnerable to Type I attacks under public-key replacement.
We analyzed the root causes of these vulnerabilities and proposed
a hash-based countermeasure that prevents algebraic manipulation.
However, our analysis reveals that fully eliminating such attacks inevitably requires hashing all public keys and random values, which
introduces a substantial computational cost, approximately 3𝑛+2 scalar
multiplications during aggregate verification.
This result indicates that, although the security of existing CLAS designs can be restored, their heavy hash-dependent structure introduces
non-negligible latency and scalability limitations in practical deployments. Therefore, an important direction for future research is to move
beyond linear hash-binding architectures and develop a new CLAS
design paradigm. Specifically, promising approaches include replacing purely linear aggregation with non-linearly bound authentication
components, such as (i) message-dependent randomness embedded in
the aggregation process, (ii) algebraically non-malleable encodings,
or (iii) lightweight zero-knowledge or challenge–response mechanisms
that break linear manipulability while preserving batch verifiability.
These approaches inherently prevent algebraic recombination attacks
and enable sublinear verification scalability in dense VANET scenarios. A concrete and fully specified CLAS construction following these
directions is left as future work.
