Efficient Pairing-Free Certificateless Signcryption
Scheme for Secure Data Transmission in IoMT
Jianhong Zhang , Chenghe Dong , and Yining Liu , Senior Member, IEEE

Abstract—The Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) builds a
bridge between patients and doctors, facilitating patients’ being
diagnosed and monitored by uploading physiological indicators
without visiting the hospital. However, physiological indicators
are sensitive data of patients, making it a challenge to achieve
verifiability of data sources while ensuring data privacy during data transmission of IoMT. Due to its ease of deployment
and the ability to provide both encryption and signature, certificateless signcryption (CLSC) is suitable for designing secure
data-transfer protocol in IoMT. Nevertheless, internal adversaries
“malicious users” and “malicious KGC,” capable of launching
Type I and Type II attacks, threaten the security of present
CLSC schemes, making most of them insecure. In this work,
after giving an example of a recent CLCS scheme suffering Type
I attack, we propose an efficient pairing-free CLCS scheme suitable for secure data transmission in IoMT based on the idea of
zero-knowledge proof. It not only provides confidentiality and
unforgeability of transmitted data under the Type I and Type II
attacks but also achieves lower computational and communication overhead, and public verifiability. Finally, compared with the
five recent CLSC schemes, theoretical analysis and experimental
testing results show that the proposed scheme outperforms the
other five schemes in terms of computation and communication
costs as well as security. Therefore, our scheme is better suited
for constructing secure data transmission in IoMT scenarios.
Index Terms—Certificateless signcryption (CLSC), confidentiality, general elliptic curve cryptography (ECC), pairing free,
unforgeability.

In 2008, Barbosa and Farshim [10] introduced the concept
of certificateless signcryption (CLSC) to overcome the above
issues.
Since then, a large number of CLSC schemes [5], [12], [13],
[14], [15], [16], [26], [27], [28], [29] have been presented and
applied in different fields, such as, the Internet of Vehicles,
5G mobile communication networks, etc. However, these
schemes either require heavy computational overhead, or fail
to provide data confidentiality or unforgeability, making them
unsuitable for application in IoMT. Medical terminal devices
in IoMT are generally very small, have limited resources,
and are often deployed in special environments that are difficult to access. This requires that these factors must be
taken into account when designing security protocols. Since
CLSC does not require certificates, it simplifies the deployment and management of secure communication in the IoMT.
Therefore, it is suitable for use in medical IoMT consisting
of resource-constrained devices. However, there are two types
of adversaries in CLSC schemes: 1) Type-I adversary and
2) Type-II adversary, both of which are internal adversaries.
Type-I adversary is considered a malicious user who can arbitrarily replace anyone’s public key, even though it does not
have a master private key. Type-II adversary is considered a
malicious key generator center (KGC) who knows the master secret key and can compute the user’s partial private key,
even though it is not allowed to replace the public keys of
the users. Most existing CLSC schemes [11], [12], [13], [14],
[15], [16], [17] are vulnerable to Type I or Type II adversaries, making them insecure. Therefore, designing a secure,
lightweight CLSC scheme for IoMT remains a challenge.
In order to design a CLSC scheme suitable for secure data
transmission in IoMT, the main contributions of the work are
outlined as follows.
1) Motivated by the aforementioned performance and security issues of existing CLSC schemes, we take a recent
IoMT-based CLSC scheme as an example, show that
the CLSC scheme is vulnerable to Type I adversary
attacks, and point out that the scheme cannot provide
the unforgeability of ciphertexts. And we give a detailed
attack.
2) We present an efficient pairing-free CLSC scheme with
public verification for securely transmitting data between
resource-constrained medical devices and data recipients
in IoMT, which provides public verification and lower
computational and communication overhead, while also
being proven to be indistinguishable and unforgeable
under the Type I and Type II attacks.
3) We evaluate the performance of the proposed scheme
by comparing it with five recent CLSC schemes. The
experimental results show that the proposed scheme outperforms the other five schemes in terms of security and
performance.

II. R ELATED W ORK
Signcryption is a cost-efficient method to achieve secure
communication in IoMT because it can achieve both signature and encryption functions in a logical-step with lowcomputational cost. In a PKI-based signcryption scheme, a
user must use its certificate to prove the legitimacy of its
public key. In an IBC-based signcryption scheme, key escrow
issues make the resource-limited medical devices even more
vulnerable in terms of security, since a malicious KGC can
generate a ciphertext in the name of the user. In 2008, to avoid
key escrow and complex certificate management, Barbosa and
Farshim [10] introduced the first CLSC system by combining
certificateless cryptography and bilinear pairing. However, the
scheme is vulnerable to malicious-but-curious KGC attacks.
To overcome the above security flaws, Liu et al. proposed a
novel CLSC scheme in [12]. Although this scheme can resist
malicious-but-curious KGC attacks, it suffers from public key
replacement attacks. In 2011, Sun and Li [11] suggested a
CLSC technique that requires just one bilinear pair operation, which was subsequently shown to have flaws. In [15],
to resist public key replacement attacks, Cheng presented an
improved CLSC scheme by introducing random factors in the
signcryption phase of the scheme [12]. Unfortunately, Cheng
and Wen [15] showed Cheng’s scheme still has security flaws,
and then put forward an improved CLSC scheme in [16]. In
2019, Luo’s scheme was found to be insecure against known
session-specific temporary information (KSSTI) attacks by
Rastegari et al., and then a practical CLSC scheme against
KSSTI attacks was proposed [14]. However, Lin et al. subsequently launched an attack on Rastegari et al.’s CLSC scheme
in [17].
To reduce computational costs to accommodate resourceconstrained devices, Zhang et al. [18] presented a lightweight
CLSC scheme. However, since the experimental environment and test results are not presented, the effectiveness
of the scheme cannot be stated. In 2019, to satisfy the
secure communication requirement of the resource-constrained
IoT devices, Karati et al. proposed a general CLSC scheme
that enables public verification with low-computational cost
in [19]. Regrettably, it was shown to fail to provide unforgeability in [9]. In 2020, Kumar et al. [20] gave a novel
CLSC scheme to achieve secure data transmission among IoT
devices, it achieve less communication overhead and computing cost. Recently, Gong et al. also presented a lightweight
CLSC scheme for IoT devices using bilinear map in [5],
However, this scheme is insecure under Type I attacks and does
not provide ciphertext unforgeability. Chen et al. [26] proposed
an efficient certificateless online/offline signcryption scheme
using online-offline technique, which reduces the computational overhead of secure data sharing between IoT devices.
Unfortunately, it is also insecure.

The schemes mentioned above incur significant communication and computation overheads due to the introduction of
time-consuming bilinear pairings. In 2008, Gopisetti et al. [21]
introduced the first efficient pairing-free CLSC scheme.
However, their scheme is unable to achieve the confidentiality of the encrypted data. In 2011, Liu and Xu also
presented a pairing-free CLSC scheme in [22], which was
subsequently found to be insecure in [32]. In 2017 and
2022, Yu and Yang [24] proposed a practical pairing-free
CLS scheme and a pairing-free certificateless aggregate signcryption scheme [30], respectively. However, both schemes
fail to satisfy their claimed security properties. Recently,
Dai and Xu [27] proposed a pairing-free CLSC scheme for
authentication of multisource data in vehicle sensor networks.
Chen et al. [29] also proposed a pairing-free CLSC scheme
for medical IoT. However, neither of these schemes is resistant to public key replacement attacks, which allow an
adversary to forge a ciphertext that can be verified by the
recipient.
Most of the existing CLSC schemes have security flaws,
as evident from the above schemes. In the CLSC scheme,
there are two types of adversaries: 1) Type I adversaries and
2) Type II adversaries. Type I adversary can replace the user’s
public key, but cannot access the KGC’s private key, while
Type II adversary has the master private key but is not permitted to replace the public key of the user. These two types
of adversaries correspond to malicious users and malicious
KGCs, respectively. Since they are internal attackers, their
attacks are very strong. Therefore, it is a challenge to design a
lightweight CLSC scheme that is resistant to Type I and Type
II attacks, to meet the requirements for secure data sharing in
IoMT networks.

IV. S YSTEM M ODEL AND S ECURITY M ODEL
A. System Model

Elliptic curve cryptography (ECC) is based on the mathematics of elliptic curves, which are a type of algebraic curve
defined by a cubic equation y2 = x3 +ax+b mod p. An elliptic
curve E defined over finite field Fp consists of all the points
(x, y) satisfying the equation y2 = x3 + ax + b mod p. If a generator Q and a prime number q satisfy the equation qP = O,
it is possible to form a cyclic addition group G consisting of
a set of points in the form dQ and an infinite point denoted
by O, where d ∈ Z.

Our system model for the IoMT is shown in Fig. 1. It contains four types of entities, and the role of each entity in the
system model are listed as follows.
1) Sensor Devices: These sensing devices are worn or
implanted in the patient’s body to sense and collect
the patient’s physiological indicators, such as heart rate,
blood oxygen content, etc., which can then be aggregated into an aggregation center (such as a mobile
phone) and then sent out.
2) KGC: KGC denotes the Key Generation Center. It is
responsible for initializing system setting and generating
the master public and private key pair. Additionally, it
is also responsible for generating partial private keys for
patient’s sensor devices and doctors.
3) Cloud Server: The cloud server in the IoMT system
has abundant storage capacity, and it is responsible for
storing the data sensed by the sensor device worn by
the patient or implanted in the body. Doctors are able
to monitor or diagnose the patients’ health status by
downloading the monitoring data from the cloud server.
4) Doctor: It is the receiver and user of the data; by analyzing the monitoring data obtained from the cloud service
center, it provides a diagnosis result.

B. Security Assumptions

B. Security Model

Computational Diffie–Hellman (CDH) Assumption: For a
cyclic addition group G with generator Q and a prime order q,
given a three-tuple (Q, d1 Q, d2 Q) ∈ G3 , it is difficult to
compute d1 d2 Q, where d1 andd2 are unknown values in Zq .
Square Diffie–Hellman (Square-DH) Assumption: For a
cyclic addition group G with generator Q and a prime order q,
given a 2-tuple (Q, dQ) ∈ G2 , it is difficult to compute d2 Q,
where d a unknown values in Zq .

In a CLSC scheme, there are two types of adversaries:
1) Type I adversaries and 2) Type II adversaries, where Type
I adversaries can replace the user’s public key, and Type II
adversaries have the master private key. In addition, since signcryption implements encryption and signature functions in one
logical step, therefore, a CLSC scheme must provide two security properties under Type I and Type II adversaries’ attacks:
1) message confidentiality and 2) ciphertext unforgeability.

C. Security Goals
Based on the system architecture and security model of the
medical Internet of Things, our scheme should satisfy four
security goals.
1) Confidentiality: The sensitive data collected by the
sensor device should be transmitted in the form of
ciphertext, and no one except the receiver can recover
the corresponding plaintext through the ciphertext.
2) Unforgeability: The unforgeability of transmitted data is
critical to ensuring the accuracy of the data. It prevents
unauthorized entities from creating a fake signcryptext, and ensures that doctors know which patient the
analyzed data comes from.
3) Public Verification: Any one can use the sender’s public key to verify the authenticity and integrity of a
signcryptext without having access to any secret keys.
4) Low-Computational Cost: Due to the limitation of the
sensor device’s own resources in IoMT, the design of
the protocol should reduce the computational complexity
and communication volume of the device as much as
possible.

VII. O UR C ONSTRUCTION
In CLSC, the form of the public key, generated by the
entity itself, is more flexible, making it challenging to design
a secure CLSC scheme that can resist Type I and Type II
attacks. To enhance the security of the CLSC scheme and
achieve secure data transmission in the IoMT. We present a
novel pairing-free CLSC scheme in this section. The proposed
scheme comprises five algorithms: 1) Setup; 2) Partial Private
Key; 3) Public/Private Key Generation; 4) Signcryption; and
5) Unsigncryption.
A. Setup
The KGC receives a security parameter λ as input, and produces the system public parameters Param = {q, G, P, Ppub ,
H1 , H2 , H3 , H4 } and master secret key s, which is securely
stored by the KGC. The process of generating the system
parameters is the same as that of the setup algorithm in the
scheme [29].
B. Partial Private Key
When provided with an identity IDi of an entity, the KGC
selects αi ∈ Zq at random and uses it to calculate Ti = αi P,
hi = H1 (IDi , Ti , Ppub ), and dIDi = αi + s · hi mod q. Finally,
the KGC returns the entity’s partial private keys (dIDi , Ti ).
C. Public/Private Key Generation
Given the system parameter Param, the entity with the identity IDi chooses a secret value xIDi ∈ Zq∗ at random, and sets
its full private key ski = (xIDi , dIDi ). And then it computes
YIDi = xIDi P, and sets its public key as pki = (YIDi , Ti ).
D. Signcryption
For a sender with identity IDs and public key pks =
(YIDs , Ts ), if it wants to send the data m to a receiver with
identity IDr and public key pkr = (YIDr , Tr ), it executes as
follows.

VIII. S ECURITY A NALYSIS
In a CLSC scheme, there are two types of adversaries:
1) Type I adversaries and 2) Type II adversaries. In the following, we demonstrate that the proposed CLSC scheme can
provide data confidentiality and unforgeability under these two
types of attacks launched by adversaries.

if an adversary breaks the IND-CCA2 security of the proposed
CLSC scheme with an advantage ξ , then we can build an
algorithm C that can utilize this adversary to solve the squareDH problem with a nonnegligible probability.

Lemma 2, which demonstrate resistance against Type 1 and
Type 2 adversaries, respectively.

C. Security
Security is the most fundamental property of a cryptographic protocol. In a secure CLSC scheme, IND-CLSCCCA2 and EUF-CLSC-CMA are the two most essential
properties to ensure its security, the former ensuring the confidentiality of the transmitted message and the latter ensuring
the unforgeability of the transmitted message. However, in
certificateless cryptography, there are two types of internal
attackers: 1) Type I attacker and 2) Type II attacker. Type
I attacker can launch attack by replacing the user’s public keys, and Type II attacker possess master secret key of
key generation center. The security overview of the proposed
scheme and other related CLSC schemes [5], [26], [27], [28],
[29] is shown in Table I. In schemes [5], [26], [27], [29],
a Type I adversary can forge a signcryptext by replacing
the user’s public keys, and the forged signcryptext can be
correctly decrypted by the receiver. Thus, the four schemes
do not resist EUC-CLSC-CMA security property under the
Type I attack. In scheme [28], for a malicious receiver, if
its public key is replaced, then the receiver can still decrypt
the corresponding plaintext. As a result, the scheme cannot satisfy the IND-CLSC-CCA security property. For the
proposed CLSC scheme, it can resist attacks from Type I and
Type II adversaries, thus providing the IND-CLSC-CCA2 and
EUC-CLSC-CMA security properties.
Security reduction in security proofs is used to establish the
level of security provided by a cryptographic scheme. This
is important because it provides a strong guarantee that the
scheme will remain secure even in the face of attacks by a
determined adversary. In the schemes [26], [27], [28], [29],
to achieve security proof of unforgeability, the corresponding security proofs are given, however, these schemes only
provide loose security reduction since they rely on the “forking lemma” technique of Pointcheval and Stern [25]. On the
other hand, both the scheme [5] and our scheme provide a
tight security reduction since they do not utilize the forking
lemma technique. It is important to note that loose security
reduction has a flaw in that even if the hard problem can be
solved efficiently, it does not necessarily mean that the corresponding cryptographic scheme can be broken efficiently. In
this regard, our scheme and the scheme [5] provide stronger
security compared to other schemes with a loose security
reduction.
X. C ONCLUSION
In IoMT, patient physiological data collected through medical terminal devices are privacy-sensitive. CLSC has the
advantages of easy deployment, low-computational overhead,
and the ability to simultaneously provide encryption and signature, making it suitable for designing secure data transmission
protocols in IoMT networks to ensure the authenticity of
patient identities and the privacy of transmitted data. However,
there are some security flaws in the existing certificate-less
sigcryption schemes, which can bring great potential risks to
the secure transmission of data in IoMT. To alleviate these
problems, we propose a new CLSC scheme capable of providing IND-CLSC-CCA2 and EUC-CLSC-CMA security under

Type I and Type II adversary attacks. Since our proposed
method does not require performing bilinear pairing operations, which makes the proposed scheme more suitable for
application in IoMT. Finally, compared to the five recent
schemes, experimental results show that our scheme outperforms all the other five schemes in terms of balancing security
and performance.
