ES-CLAS: An Efficient Cerificateless Fully
Aggregate Signature Scheme for
Vehicular Ad Hoc Networks
Qi Tao

and Xiaohui Cui , Senior Member, IEEE

Abstract—In this work, it reviewed the recent authentication
scheme based on certificateless aggregate signature (CLAS) in
vehicular ad hoc network (VANET). The previously proposed
scheme solved a few of the security problems in VANETs, but
it cann’t resist forgery attacks. Besides, the computation and
communication cost is not sufficiently low that would affect the
efficient delivery of emergency traffic messages. In this paper, we
propose a new efficient cerificateless fully aggregate signature
scheme for VANETs. The proposed scheme used the general
one-way hash function operations instead of the expensive mapto-point hash functions. It inherits the characteristics of CLAS
by aggregating various message signatures from different vehicles
into a single signature, thus reducing the computation and
communication overhead of the scheme. A lightweight conditional
traceability method is designed to enable the traffic management
department to reveal the real identity of malicious vehicles. Based
on elliptic curve discrete logarithm (ECDL) problem, it proved
that the proposed scheme is existential unforgeability under
the adaptive chosen-message attack and satisfies security goals.
Performance analysis shows that our scheme is more efficient than
similar schemes in terms of computation and communication
overhead. It will reduce at least 24.3% in computation cost than
compared schemes.
Index Terms—Vehicular ad hoc network, aggregate signature,
privacy protection, traceability, smart transportation.

I. I NTRODUCTION
EHICULAR ad hoc network (VANET) is an important
basic infrastructure to support the development of intelligent vehicles. With the gradual maturity of 5G technology
and satellite Internet technology, the landing application of
Internet of Vehicles technology has entered a stage of rapid
development. It could realize the all-around network connection between the vehicle and the pedestrian, the vehicle and the
vehicle, the vehicle and the infrastructure, and the vehicle and
the service network, so as to improve the autonomous driving
ability and promote the application of autonomous driving [1].

A typical VANET includes On-Board Unit (OBU) embedded
in the vehicle, Roadside Unit (RSU) installed alongside the
road, Key Generation Center(KGC) and Trusted Authority(TA)
[2] as shown in Fig. 1.
In the VANET, based on the Dedicated Short Range Communication (DSRC) protocol, the OBUs and the RSUs would
self-organize to form a multi-hop communication network
[3]. It provides an effective channel to transmit safety information and avoid traffic accidents [4]. Through the sensor
device, wireless communication device, GPS, camera, and
other equipment installed inside the vehicle, the OBU device
can collect its state information and peripheral environmental
information, and share information with other vehicles or
nearby RSUs based on DSRC protocol [5]. RSU transmits the
traffic information collected within the communication area to
the local Traffic Management Center (TMC) by the optical
fiber communication network. Based on the collected traffic
information, the TMC makes intelligent analyses and decisions
on traffic conditions and feedback on the decision information
to each vehicle. In this way, it would realize the functions of
emergency warning and route planning.
However, with the persistent penetration of the VANET
system by the ubiquitous and open communication standards
of wireless communication and network attacks of traditional

II. R ELATED W ORK
Cryptography is an effective solution to provide security
for data in the VANET network. Shamir [16] introduced
an identity-based public key cryptosystem (ID-PKC). In this
scheme, any user is free to choose the public key, while the
user’s private key is issued from the private key generator
(PKI). Some ID-PKC schemes have been proposed [17], [18].
In the ID-PKC schemes, since the PKI generated the user’s
private key, the possibility of malicious attacks cannot be
ruled out for PKI, which could be faked as legitimate users
sending messages. Traditional cryptosystems are facing serious
challenges of key escrow [13].
Certificateless signature scheme (CLS) provides a method
for the key escrow challenges. The certificateless aggregate
signature(CLAS) scheme was firstly introduced by Al-Riyami
and Paterson [5] in 2003, which avoided the overhead of
certificate management by solving the key escrow problem
in the ID-PKC system. However, Huang et al. [19] proved the
scheme [5] is difficult to resist forgery attacks under its own
defined security model. Later, several CLAS schemes were
described from the view of enhanced security or increased
efficiency [20], [21], [22]. Shen et al. [21] discovered that the
scheme [20] was insecure through concrete attacks on adversary A1 and adversary A2, where adversary A1 would replace
the user’s public key but couldn’t access the KGC’s (Key Generation Center, KGC) secret key and adversary A2 has access
the KGC’s secret key but couldn’t change the user’s public
key. Kumar et al. [22] introduced a pairing-based secure CLAS
scheme in VANETS under the Computational Diffie-Hellman
assumption and the random oracle model. Zhong et al. [23]
presented an efficient CLAS-based authentication scheme
for vehicle-to-infrastructure communication. In their scheme,
pseudonyms are used to achieve conditional privacy protection. But, it proved that the scheme [23] was vulnerable to
attacks from the compromised KGC, and an adversary could

B. System Model
There are five entities in the proposed system model, as
shown in Fig. 2, including the Trusted Authority (TA), Traffic
Management Center (TMC) server, the Key Generation Center
(KGC), Roadside Units (RSUs), and vehicles installed with
OBU. The system model for VANET consists of an application layer and a management layer. The management layer
includes TA, KGC, and TMC. The application layer includes
RSU devices and vehicles, which communicated with the
management layer through a secure fiber optic communication
network. The brief details of each entity are shown below.
• TA. TA is a trusted management device. Vehicles and
RSUs in VANETs are registered and managed through
TA. The TA server communicated with RSU devices by
the wired transport layer security protocol. Vehicles and
RSU devices should be registered with TA before an operation, then the TA will generate pseudonyms for them.
So, vehicles can transmit messages with pseudonyms to
protect data privacy and message integrity. It requires
the TA should have powerful computation and enough
storage memory, and usually set multiple TA to avoid
performance bottlenecks.
• TMC. TMC is responsible for providing navigation,
vehicle traceability, intelligent traffic control, entertainment, traffic rescue, and other services. It collected traffic
messages from RSUs by a secure channel.
• KGC. KGC is a semi-trusted device. It is used to generate
the partial private key for vehicles in VANETs. It requires
the KGC should have powerful computation and usually
set multiple KGCs to avoid performance bottlenecks.
• RSUs. The RSUs are wireless communication devices.
It is installed on the infrastructure along the roadside at
special intervals. They have distributed all over the system
network, serving as bridges between the management
layer and the application layer. RSU devices can verify
the received messages and transmit the process result
to application layer devices for data analysis. To ensure
RSUs help vehicles verify messages, it assumes RSU
devices are semi-trusted and collect and mine vehicle
information out of curiosity but wouldn’t refuse message
verification.

key λ. The KGC and TA select a cyclic group G. Then
publish the system parameters. The vehicle Vi and RSU

The ES-CLAS scheme should satisfy the following security
requirements [30], [31], [32]. The next will introduce the
security objectives of the proposed scheme, as follows.
• Message integrity and user anonymity: The ES-CLAS
scheme can check the shared message’s integrity to
prevent the adversary from changing it. At the same time,
it could preserve the privacy of the vehicle’s users, even
if an adversary intercepts the communications as they are
being transmitted, the user’s real identity and behavior
cannot be discovered.
• Unlinkability: In the ES-CLAS scheme, RSUs and malicious vehicles are unable to link messages delivered by
the same vehicle to safeguard privacy.
• Traceability: TMC can discover the vehicle’s real identity
from the accident messages, preventing malicious vehicles from denying their responsibility for traffic accidents.
• Resistance to attacks: The ES-CLAS scheme should resist
the basic attacks, including the Man-in-the-Middle attack,
the replay attack, the Sybil attack and the modification
attack.

V. P ROPOSED ES-CLAS S CHEME
In this section, we introduce a new efficient and secure ESCLAS scheme for VANETs.
A. System Architecture
The system operation details of the proposed scheme are
shown in Fig. 3, as follows.
1) According to traffic regulations, each vehicle should be
registered with the TMC before it is driven on the road,
so as to track the vehicle and identity the owner in case
of special event(e.g. accident, traffic jam, etc.).
2) During system initialization, KGC and TA select a
random secret key s and a secret trace key b respectively,
and generate the system public key. The TA then sends
b to the TMC over an SSL secure channel.
3) When vehicles first enter the VANETs, the vehicle’s
OBU device will communicate with the nearby RSU
device via a 20Mbps bandwith based on IEEE 802.11P
protocol [33], and will submit the registration information (PID1 , AID).

databases to obtain the owner information.

B. The Proposed Scheme
The proposed ES-CLAS scheme contains the following
eight algorithms.
• Initialization. The system inputs a secret key λ. Key
Generation Center (KGC) and Trusted Authority(TA)
select a q-order cyclic group G with a generator P. KGC
chooses a secret key s ∈ Zq? and calculates P pub = sP. TA
selects a secret trace key b ∈ Zq? and outputs T pub = bP.
KGC and TA select two secure general one-way hash
function H1 : {0, 1}? × G × G → Zq? , H2 : {0, 1}? ×
{0, 1}? × G → Zq? . Then publish the system parameters (P, q, E, G, H1 , H2 , P pub , T pub , Zq? ). The vehicle Vi and
RSU send its identity RID to TA for registration. TA sends
the trace key b to the Traffic Management Center (TMC).
• Pseudo-identity generation. Vehicle Vi chooses a random param ti ∈ Zq? , calculates {PID1 = ti P, AIDi =

This section would discuss the ES-CLAS scheme meeting
the necessary security requirements introduced in section III

1) Message integrity and user anonymity: As proof
of Theorem 1, it is hard for the adversary with
advantage ε0 = (qH qHε qvsk ) to deal with the ECDL
1
2
assumption in probability polynomial time. Since the
ES-CLAS scheme is secure to defend against message forgery under adaptive selective-message attacks, it
can achieve message integrity by checking the validity
of the message verification equation (6). In addition,
since the vehicle’s pseudo-identity PID = {PID1 =
ti P, PID2 = RIDi ⊕ H1 (ti T pub , PID1 , 4T )} satisfied with
ECDL assumption, so the adversary couldn’t deduce the
real identity RID from PID2 . Hence, the proposed ESCLAS scheme for VANETs has the characteristics of
message integrity and vehicle anonymity.
2) Unlinkability: In our proposed scheme, the verifier
sends signature {PID, V PK, m, T , X, sg} to nearby RSU.
Due to the signature sg is generated based on the

freshness timestamp T and a random secret param xi , the
value of each xi for each sg is unique. So, no adversary
can connect the two signatures on the same vehicle. The
ES-CLAS scheme is unlinkability.
3) Traceability: Anonymity protects the vehicle’s trajectory from attackers. However, in certain circumstances
(likely accidents and traffic jams), the vehicle’s real
identity should be retrieved by the traffic management department. This conditional traceability enable
the TMC to discover the vehicle’s real identity RID
based on the traceability algorithm in section V, where
RID = PID2 ⊕ H1 (bPID1 , PID1 , 4T ). It is useful for
the traffic management department to track and prevent
vehicles from denying their responsibility for traffic
accidents. Hence, the ES-CLAS scheme only provides
TMC with tracking service for malicious vehicles.
4) Resistance to attacks: Due to Theorem 1, the proposed
ES-CLAS scheme can resist basic attacks, including
the Man-in-the-Middle attack, the replay attack, and
the modification attack. Even though the KGC is compromised, the adversary can’t forge a valid signature,
because the secret key xi is a random param that is hard
to deduce from Xi .
• Resistance to Man-in-the-Middle Attack. Assume an
adversary has got a vehicle’s pseudo-identity PID,
and attempts to impersonate a vehicle to forge a
valid message signature {m, sg, V PK = (Ui ), Xi , T }.
It should deduce the secret params (xi , Ri ), so it
firstly gets the xi from Xi , and Ri from Ui . According
to the analysis of Theorem 1, it is hard for the
adversary to solve the ECDL problem to get (xi , Ri ).
So, the proposed scheme can resistance to Man-inthe-Middle attacks.
• Resistance to Replay Attack. In the proposed
scheme, the freshness timestamp T is used to generate a signature. It can resistance the message replay
attacks by verifying whether equation (6) is held.
• Resistance to Sybil Attack. According to the formal
proof (Theorems 1, 2 and 3) of the proposed scheme
in Section VI-A, it is difficult for adversary to impersonate other legitimate vehicles to forge a valid
signature based on the ECDL assumption under the
random oracle model. The proposed scheme has
anti-forgery security under adaptive chosen-message
attack, type I attack and type II attack. So, the
proposed scheme can resistance to Sybil attacks.
• Resistance to Modification Attack. As proof of
Theorem 1, the adversary can’t forge a valid message signature. The proposed system would accept
the shared traffic message only if equation (6) is
valid. So, the proposed scheme can resistance to
modification attacks.

In this paper, we analyzed Thumbur et al’s scheme in
VANETs and proved that the scheme is insecure to defend
against impersonation attacks. Besides, we constructed a new
efficient cerificateless fully aggregate signature scheme for
VANETs. The proposed ES-CLAS scheme implements message authentication and integrity, unlinkability, nonrepudiation
but vehicle anonymity, and traceability. Moreover, we proved
that the ES-CLAS scheme is secure against forgery under the
random oracle model. In addition, compared with the existing
relevant schemes, the signature and verification overhead of
ES-CLAS scheme decreased by about 24.3% and 48.9%
respectively. Communication cost decreased by about 19.2%.
Our future work will explore to design authentication
schemes for VANETs with different functions, including aggregate signature based on smart contract, group
signature, etc.
