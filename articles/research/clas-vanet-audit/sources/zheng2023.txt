  A Security-Enhanced Pairing-Free Certificateless
 Aggregate Signature for Vehicular Ad-Hoc Networks
                            Hangcheng Zheng, Min Luo , Yunru Zhang , Cong Peng , and Qi Feng


    Abstract—As self-organizing, highly heterogeneous and multi-
interactive networks, vehicular ad-hoc networks (VANETs) are
rapidly improving the efficiency of modern traffic. This promising
technology is significant for supporting current intelligent trans-
portation by cooperative data sharing and processing. Consid-
ering requirements for secure interactions and the limitation of
resources in VANETs, protecting data authenticity while enhancing
computational efficiency has become the main challenge. A cer-
tificateless aggregate signature (CLAS) can not only ensure data
security, but also reduce communication costs, which is suitable
for a resource-constrained environment. However, most existing
CLAS schemes either own serious security vulnerabilities or re-
sult in tremendous computational overhead. In this article, we
propose an efficient, privacy-preserving, and pairing-free CLAS
scheme with enhanced security. We prove that it is existentially
unforgeable against adaptive chosen message attacks in the random
oracle model. Performance evaluation illustrates that our proposed
scheme is more appropriate to the VANETs scenario.
   Index Terms—Certificateless aggregate signature (CLAS),
digital signature scheme, provably security, vehicular ad-hoc
networks.

                            II. RELATED WORK                                                                          TABLE I

   The basic conception of aggregate signature was first pro-
posed in [3] by Boneh et al. in 2003. After the notion was
introduced, numerous state-of-the-art cryptographic schemes
were raised. Generally speaking, traditional PKI-based schemes
(see [10], [11], [12], [13], [14]) bind the public key with a user’s
identity through a certificate issued by the certificate authority
(CA). This may bring about vast overhead caused by the man-
agement of certificates when the amount of users increases. To
solve this problem, scholars and academicians began to consider
schemes based on identity. Gentry et al. [15] put forward the
first identity-based aggregate signature scheme. After this work,
abundant identity-based schemes (see [16], [17], [18], [19]) were
proposed. However, the private key is completely generated by
KGC in an identity-based cryptosystem, which may lead to
serious key escrow problems.
   Considering the above problems of schemes based on PKI
and IBC cryptosystem, the notion of certificateless public-key
cryptography [2] (CL-PKC) was raised. In CL-PKC, the private
key is made up of two components: a secret value chosen by the
user as well as a partial private key produced by KGC. It is able
to eliminate the certificate distribution and key escrow problem
in PKC and IBC cryptosystem to a certain extent.
   After the first CLAS scheme [4] was presented in 2007,
a series of CLAS schemes were proposed. Xiong et al. [20]
designed a certificateless signature and raised a CLAS scheme.
