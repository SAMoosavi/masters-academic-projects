# A New Conditional Privacy-Preserving CLAS in the Standard Model for VANETs

## Overview

This paper cryptanalyses the Wang et al. conditional privacy-preserving CLAS scheme and proposes an improved CLAS scheme with a standard-model security proof and performance evaluation (p.1).

## Relevance to Our Work

Bib verified: Mathematics 2023, 11, 4766, DOI 10.3390/math11234766, 18 pages, open access (CC BY) (p.1). Standard-model claim verified: the improved scheme is proven unforgeable in the standard model under CDHP against adversary A1 (Theorem 1) and adversary A2 (Theorem 2) (p.10–12).

## Key Contributions

- Cryptanalysis of Wang et al.'s scheme showing logical incorrectness plus vulnerability to KGC forgery and public-key replacement attacks (p.6–7).
- Improved eight-stage CLAS construction with new system key Z = H3(y_pub) and hash families H1–H5 (p.7–8).
- Standard-model formal proof reducing forgery by A1/A2 to CDHP via Lemmas 1–2 and Theorems 1–2 (p.10–12).
- Informal analysis of seven VANET properties plus computation, communication, and RSU-capacity evaluation (p.12–15).

## Mathematical Notation

Source tables define q, G1/G2, e, P/Q, params, Ppub/s, H1–H3, pseudonyms, keys, and signatures for the reviewed scheme (p.5).

### Setup (improved scheme)

$$
\begin{aligned}
&e: G_1 \times G_1 \to G_2,\ q > 2^{\nu} \\
&y_{pub} = sP,\quad Z = H_3(y_{pub}) \\
&params = \lbrace G_1, G_2, q, e, P, Z, y_{pub}, H_1, H_2, H_3, H_4, H_5 \rbrace
\end{aligned}
$$

- KGC picks P in G1 and s in Zq* with y_pub = sP and Z = H3(y_pub); TRA picks k with K = kP (p.8).
- Hash domains are H1: G1 -> Zq*, H2: {0,1}* x G1 x G1 -> Zq*, H3: G1 -> G1, H4: G1^4 -> Zq*, H5: {0,1}* x {0,1}* x G1^5 -> Zq* (p.8).

### Partial Private Key Extract

$$
\begin{aligned}
&r_i \in \mathbb{Z}_q^*,\quad R_i = r_i P \\
&k_i = H_2(PID_{i,j}, R_i) \\
&d_i = r_i + k_i \cdot s \bmod q
\end{aligned}
$$

- KGC selects r_i, computes R_i = r_i P, k_i = H2(PIDi,j, R_i), and d_i = r_i + k_i s mod q as the partial private key (p.8).

### Set Secret Value / Keys

$$
\begin{aligned}
&x_i \in \mathbb{Z}_q^*,\quad X_i = x_i P \\
&PK_i = (X_i, R_i),\quad \text{private key } (d_i, x_i)
\end{aligned}
$$

- Vehicle picks secret x_i, computes X_i = x_i P, publishes PKi = (X_i, R_i), and keeps (d_i, x_i) as the private key (p.8).

### Signature (improved scheme)

$$
\begin{aligned}
&u_i \in \mathbb{Z}_q^*,\quad U_i = u_i P \\
&\varphi_i = H_4(y_{pub}, PK_i, U_i) \\
&h_i = H_5(m_i \| TS_i, PID_{i,j}, U_i, PK_i) \\
&W_i = (d_i \cdot \varphi_i + h_i \cdot x_i + u_i) \cdot Z \\
&\sigma_i = (U_i, W_i)
\end{aligned}
$$

- Signer picks timestamp TSi and random u_i with U_i = u_i P, then computes phi_i, h_i, and W_i = (d_i phi_i + h_i x_i + u_i) Z (p.8).

### Aggregate (CLAS)

$$
\begin{aligned}
&U = \sum_{i=1}^n U_i,\quad W = \sum_{i=1}^n W_i \\
&\sigma = (U, W)
\end{aligned}
$$

- RSU sums n individual pairs into U = sum U_i and W = sum W_i and sends sigma = (U, W) to the TMC (p.9).

### Verification

$$
\begin{aligned}
&e(W_i, P) = e((R_i + k_i \cdot y_{pub}) \cdot \varphi_i + h_i \cdot X_i + U_i, Z)
\end{aligned}
$$

- Single-signature check is equation (3) after recomputing k_i, phi_i, and h_i and testing timestamp freshness (p.8).
- Aggregate check is e(W,P) = e(sum (R_i + k_i y_pub) phi_i + sum h_i X_i + U, Z) as equation (4) (p.9).

## Protocol / Scheme

1. **Setup plus registration**: five entities (KGC, TRA, OBU, RSU, TMC) with pseudonym PIDi,j = (PIDi,1,j, Ti,j) and TRA-only traceability via k (p.3–4, p.8).
2. **Key issue**: KGC issues d_i over a trusted route and the vehicle forms PKi = (X_i, R_i) with secret x_i (p.8).
3. **Sign**: OBU binds message, timestamp, pseudonym, U_i, and PKi into phi_i/h_i and outputs (U_i, W_i) with single-use pseudonyms (p.8).
4. **Single verify**: RSU checks timestamp freshness and equation (3) before accepting (p.8).
5. **Aggregate and aggregate-verify**: RSU sums to (U, W) and TMC checks freshness plus equation (4) (p.9).
6. **Prior-scheme review**: Wang et al. used params with P, Q, Ppub and H1–H3, signatures (U_i, V_i, W_i), and equations (1)–(2) (p.5–6).

## Security Analysis

- Wang et al. signing is logically incorrect because h_i hashes W_i before W_i is computed; the fix hashes without W_i (p.6).
- KGC forgery works because KGC knows l with Q = lP and forges W_i0 = l(R_i + k_i Ppub + h_i0 X_i + U_i0) passing equation (1) (p.6–7).
- Public-key replacement works by replacing X_i with X_i0 = x_i0 P and setting U_i0 = u_i0 P - (R_i + k_i Ppub) with W_i0 = (h_i0 x_i0 + u_i0) Q (p.7).
- Correctness of equations (3) and (4) is derived by expanding d_i = r_i + k_i s and moving Z across the pairing (p.9).
- Formal result: Theorems 1–2 state unforgeability in the standard model under CDHP against A1 (external, key-replacing) and A2 (internal, master-key-holding) (p.10–12).
- Informal result: the paper claims authentication, nonrepudiation, anonymity, unlinkability, traceability, anti-replay, and anti-impersonation (p.12–13).

## Performance

- Testbed is an i5-11300H 3.11 GHz, 16 GB RAM desktop using Java with Java.security and JPBC for pairing operations (p.13).
- Signing costs 2Tmul + 2Th ~= 2.3046 ms, reported as the lowest among the six compared schemes (p.13–14).
- Single verification costs 2Tbp + 3Tmul + 3Tpa + 3Th ~= 24.1875 ms, less than four comparators but slightly more than Wang et al. at 23.0352 ms (p.13–14).
- Aggregate verification costs 2Tbp + 3nTmul + 3nTpa + 3nTh ~= 3.5397n + 20.6478 ms with scaling plotted against signature count (p.13–14).
- Communication is 2|G1| + timestamp = 260 bytes per signature and 2|G1| + n timestamps = 656 bytes at n = 100, equal to Kamil et al. and below the other comparators (p.14–15).
- RSU capacity uses Rsc = p d / (Tver N v) with coverage d = 1000 m, N vehicles in 800 m, speed 5–20 m/s, and Tver stated as 34.0827 ms (p.15).

## Strengths & Limitations

- Strength: explicit KGC-attack and replacement-attack demonstrations with pairing equations rather than assertions alone (p.6–7).
- Strength: standard-model reduction structure for both adversary types with CDHP extraction formulas for single and aggregate forgery (p.10–12).
- Limitation: corrupted-signature localization still needs one-by-one or bisection search plus a penalty mechanism for repeat offenders (p.9).
- Limitation: aggregate verification grows with 3n multiplications, additions, and hashes, so batch cost rises linearly in n (p.13–14).
- Interpretation: the Tver value in the Rsc paragraph differs from the Table 4 single-verification figure, so reuse the stated Rsc inputs with care (p.14–15).
