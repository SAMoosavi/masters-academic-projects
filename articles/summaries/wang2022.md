# A Conditional Privacy-Preserving Certificateless Aggregate Signature Scheme in the Standard Model for VANETs

## Overview

This paper proposes a conditional privacy-preserving certificateless aggregate signature (CLAS) scheme for VANETs whose security is formally proven in the standard model (SM) rather than the random oracle model (ROM), on the grounds that ROM-proven schemes may be insecure in actual implementation (p.1, Abstract; p.2, §I).

It adopts full aggregation so the RSU compresses n vehicle signatures into one aggregate verified once by the TMC, and uses a TRA-issued pseudonym mechanism for conditional privacy, with formal unforgeability proofs under the computational Diffie-Hellman problem (CDHP) (p.1, Abstract; p.6, §V.G–H).

## Relevance to Our Work

Directly relevant as the SM-baseline for our comparison: it claims a stronger real-world guarantee than ROM-based CLAS schemes and must be compared on both security model (SM vs ROM) and performance (p.1, Abstract; p.9, §VII.A).

Interpretation: its "two pairings, no map-to-point" aggregate verification is the efficiency claim our audit must test against the paper's own cost tables (p.2, §I; p.10, §VII.B).

## Key Contributions

- Proposes a conditional privacy-preserving CLAS scheme for V2I communication in VANETs with claimed integrity, non-repudiation, anonymity, unlinkability, traceability, and replay-attack resistance (p.2, §I).
- Proves existential unforgeability under adaptive chosen-message attacks (EUF-CMA) against Type I and Type II (malicious-but-passive KGC) adversaries in the SM under CDHP, claimed as the first such conditional privacy-preserving CLAS for VANETs (p.2, §I; p.7–8, §VI.A).
- Uses full aggregation to cut computation and bandwidth, requiring only two bilinear pairings and no map-to-point operations for aggregate verification (p.1, Abstract; p.2, §I; p.10, §VII.B).
- Realizes conditional privacy via TRA pseudonyms: vehicles communicate under `PIDi,j` while only the TRA can recover the true `IDi` with tracking key `k` (p.1, Abstract; p.6, §V.B).
- Reports lower computation and communication overhead than several previous CLAS schemes, supported by MIRACL timings and a practicability (RSU service capacity) evaluation (p.1, Abstract; p.10–12, §VII.B–D).

## Mathematical Notation

### Setup

Setup parameters below are the paper's published `params = {G1, G2, q, e, P, Q, Ppub, H1, H2, H3}` with KGC master secret `s` and TRA tracking key `k` kept secret (p.6, §V.A).

$$
\begin{aligned}
&G_1, G_2: \text{cyclic groups of prime order } q > 2^{\nu} \\
&P, Q \in G_1,\quad e: G_1 \times G_1 \to G_2 \\
&s \in \mathbb{Z}_q^*,\quad P_{pub} = sP \quad (\text{KGC master key}) \\
&k \in \mathbb{Z}_q^*,\quad K = kP \quad (\text{TRA tracking key}) \\
&H_1: G_1 \to \mathbb{Z}_q^*,\quad H_2: \{0,1\}^* \times G_1 \times G_1 \to \mathbb{Z}_q^* \\
&H_3: \{0,1\}^* \times \{0,1\}^* \times G_1^6 \to \mathbb{Z}_q^*
\end{aligned}
$$

KGC picks `P, Q, s` with `Ppub = sP`, TRA picks `k` with `K = kP`, and they publish `params` while `s, k` stay secret (p.6, §V.A).

### Pseudonym generation

$$
\begin{aligned}
&t_{i,j} \in_R \mathbb{Z}_q^*,\quad T_{i,j} = t_{i,j}P \\
&PID_{i,1,j} = ID_i \oplus H_1(kP + T_{i,j}),\quad PID_{i,j} = \{PID_{i,1,j}, T_{i,j}\}
\end{aligned}
$$

TRA assigns each vehicle a pseudonym `PIDi,j` from its true identity `IDi`, and recovers `IDi = PIDi,1,j ⊕ H1(kP + Ti,j)` with tracking key `k` when tracing malicious vehicles (p.6, §V.B).

### Partial Private Key Extract

$$
\begin{aligned}
&r_i \in_R \mathbb{Z}_q^*,\quad R_i = r_i P \\
&k_i = H_2(PID_{i,j}, R_i) \\
&d_i = r_i + k_i \cdot s \bmod q
\end{aligned}
$$

KGC generates the partial private key `di` as above and sends it to the vehicle via SSL (p.6, §V.C).

### Set Secret Value and keys

$$
\begin{aligned}
&x_i \in_R \mathbb{Z}_q^*,\quad X_i = x_i P \\
&PK_i = (X_i, R_i),\quad SK_i = (d_i, x_i)
\end{aligned}
$$

The vehicle picks secret `xi`, sets `Xi = xiP`, so its public key is `PKi = (Xi, Ri)` and full private key is `(di, xi)` (p.6, §V.D).

### Signature

$$
\begin{aligned}
&u_i \in_R \mathbb{Z}_q^*,\quad U_i = u_i P,\quad V_i = u_i Q \\
&h_i = H_3(m_i \Vert TS_i, PID_{i,j}, U_i, V_i, W_i, PK_i) \\
&W_i = (d_i + h_i \cdot x_i) Q + V_i,\quad \sigma_i = (U_i, V_i, W_i)
\end{aligned}
$$

The OBU picks timestamp `TSi` and randomness `ui`, computes the above, and sends `(mi, TSi, PKi, PIDi,j, Ui, Vi, Wi)` to the RSU, with each pseudonym used once (p.6, §V.E).

### Aggregate

$$
\begin{aligned}
&U = \sum_{i=1}^n U_i,\quad V = \sum_{i=1}^n V_i,\quad W = \sum_{i=1}^n W_i \\
&\sigma = (U, V, W)
\end{aligned}
$$

The RSU sums the n components and forwards the aggregate `σ = (U, V, W)` to the TMC (p.6, §V.G).

### Verification

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{Single:}\quad e(W_i, P) \stackrel{?}{=} e(R_i + k_i P_{pub} + h_i X_i + U_i, Q) \\
&\text{Aggregate:}\quad e(W, P) \stackrel{?}{=} e\left(\sum_{i=1}^n R_i + \sum_{i=1}^n k_i P_{pub} + \sum_{i=1}^n h_i X_i + U, Q\right)
\end{aligned}
$$

The RSU checks freshness of `TSi` then equation (1) for single signatures, and the TMC checks freshness of all `TSi` then equation (2) for the aggregate (p.6, §V.F, §V.H).

## Protocol / Scheme

1. **Setup**: KGC selects `P, Q, s` with `Ppub = sP`, TRA selects `k` with `K = kP`, and they publish `params = {G1, G2, q, e, P, Q, Ppub, H1, H2, H3}` (p.6, §V.A).
2. **Pseudonym Generation**: vehicle sends `(IDi, Ti,j)` to TRA, which returns `PIDi,j = {IDi ⊕ H1(kP + Ti,j), Ti,j}` (p.6, §V.B).
3. **Partial Private Key Generation**: KGC picks `ri`, computes `Ri = riP`, `ki = H2(PIDi,j, Ri)`, `di = ri + ki·s mod q`, and sends `di` via SSL (p.6, §V.C).
4. **Key Generation**: vehicle picks `xi`, sets `Xi = xiP`, with `PKi = (Xi, Ri)` and `(di, xi)` as private key (p.6, §V.D).
5. **Sign**: vehicle picks `TSi, ui`, computes `Ui, Vi, hi, Wi` as in the Signature box, and outputs `σi = (Ui, Vi, Wi)` (p.6, §V.E).
6. **Single Verification**: RSU checks `TSi` freshness, recomputes `ki, hi`, and checks equation (1) (p.6, §V.F).
7. **Aggregate**: RSU sums components into `σ = (U, V, W)` and sends it to the TMC (p.6, §V.G).
8. **Aggregate Verification**: TMC checks all `TSi`, recomputes `ki, hi`, and checks equation (2) (p.6, §V.H).

## Security Analysis

- Proves EUF-CMA against Type I adversaries (Theorem 1/Lemma 1: public-key replacement, no master secret) in the SM by reduction to CDHP (p.7, §VI.A).
- Proves EUF-CMA against Type II adversaries, i.e. malicious-but-passive KGC holding master secret `s` (Theorem 2/Lemma 2), in the SM by reduction to CDHP (p.8, §VI.A).
- Claims authentication/integrity via the EUF-CMA proofs and aggregate verification, non-repudiation via TRA traceability, anonymity via pseudonyms, unlinkability via fresh per-message pseudonyms, and traceability by TRA-only `IDi` recovery (p.8–9, §VI.B).
- Claims replay-attack resistance via timestamps `TSi` checked by RSU/TMC before verification (p.8, §VI.B).
- Security-attribute comparison (Table 2) reports this scheme plus [15] and [20] satisfy all listed attributes (resist AI/AII, authentication, anonymity, unlinkability, traceability, replay resistance), while the others satisfy only some, and notes the compared schemes are ROM-proven whereas this one is SM-proven (p.9, §VII.A).

## Performance

- No map-to-point operations anywhere; signing costs `3Tmul + Tpa + Th ≈ 5.8518 ms` (p.10, §VII.B).
- Single-signature verification costs `2Tbp + 2Tmul + 3Tpa + 2Th ≈ 34.0828 ms` with only two pairings (p.10, §VII.B).
- Aggregate verification costs `2Tbp + 2nTmul + 3nTpa + 2nTh ≈ 3.9352n + 30.1476 ms`, also with only two pairings, scaling linearly and reported below [9], [15], [18]–[22] (p.10, §VII.B).
- Communication (G1 = 128 bytes, timestamp = 4 bytes): single signature `3×128 + 4 = 388` bytes, aggregate `3×128 + 4n = 784` bytes at n = 100 — lower than [9], [18], [19], [21], [22] but slightly larger than [15] and [20] (`656` bytes), traded against lower computation and SM proofs (p.11–12, §VII.C).
- Practicability: RSU service capacity `Rsc = p·d/(Tver·N·v)` with `Tver = 34.0828 ms`, d = 1000 m, N = 600–800 m, v = 5–20 m/s; Rsc falls with density/speed and the RSU verifies about eight signatures per 300 ms (p.12, §VII.D).

## Strengths & Limitations

- Strength: SM security proofs (Theorems 1–2 under CDHP) give a stronger claimed real-world guarantee than the ROM-based comparators, with only two pairings and zero map-to-point operations (p.2, §I; p.9–10, §VII.A–B).
- Strength: full aggregation plus pseudonym mechanism jointly address bandwidth and conditional privacy (traceable anonymity/unlinkability) in one construction (p.1, Abstract; p.6, §V.B, §V.G).
- Limitation: aggregate signatures are slightly larger than [15] and [20] (784 vs 656 bytes at n = 100), which the authors acknowledge while arguing computation and SM security compensate (p.12, §VII.C).
- Limitation: per-message timestamps are the sole stated replay defense, so correctness depends on clock synchronization and freshness windows enforced by RSU/TMC, as described by the authors (p.6, §V.F, §V.H; p.8, §VI.B).
- Limitation: evaluation is analytic (MIRACL timings plus Rsc formula) rather than large-scale deployment measurement, per the reported method (p.9–12, §VII.B–D).

## Related Work

- Design rationale is stated as combining Mei et al. [15] (conditional privacy-preserving CLAS requiring four pairings in aggregate verification, ROM-proven) with Deng et al. [38] (first SM-proven CLAS, lacking replay-attack resistance and anonymity for VANETs) (p.3, §III).
- Performance comparators are prior CLAS schemes [9], [15], [18]–[22] evaluated in Tables 4–5 and Figs. 5–7 for computation and communication overhead (p.10–11, §VII.B–C).
