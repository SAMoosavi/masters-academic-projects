# An Efficient and Secure CLAS for VANETs (HECC-based)

## Overview

This paper proposes a certificateless aggregate signature (CLAS) scheme for vehicle-to-RSU authentication built on hyperelliptic curve cryptography (HECC, genus 2) instead of ECC or bilinear pairings, targeting lower computation and communication cost at an 80-bit key size (Abstract, p.1; §3, p.5). Vehicles request partial private keys over an open (insecure) channel using encrypted identities, so the scheme provides sender and receiver anonymity and removes the secure-channel assumption of classic certificateless key issuance (Abstract, p.1; §5 PRPKG, p.7). Signatures are generated by the vehicle's OBU and verified by the RSU via a divisor-scalar-multiplication equation, with aggregation performed as in Han et al. eCLAS [29] (§5 SIGG/SIGV, pp.7–8). Security is proven as existential unforgeability against Type 1 (FGR1) and Type 2 (FGR2) forgers in the random oracle model under the hyperelliptic curve discrete logarithm problem (Abstract, p.1; Theorems 1–2, pp.9–11).

## Relevance to Our Work

This is the HECC-based reference design for our CLAS-for-VANET audit: it replaces ECDLP/CDH with the hyperelliptic-curve discrete logarithm (HDLP) assumption, keeps the ROM + Type I/II proof structure, and reports the best computation (1.92 ms total) and communication (1160 bits) figures among its comparators. Its encrypted-identity mechanism for open-channel partial-key delivery is the concrete realization of the SA/RA/PPK properties that competing schemes lack (§5 PRPKG, p.7; §6.1, Table 2, p.12).

## Key Contributions

- Proposes a pairing-free CLAS over HECC (genus-2 curve, 80-bit divisor) for VANET vehicle authentication (§5, Fig. 3, pp.6–8)
- Achieves sender and receiver anonymity via encrypted identities plus partial-key delivery over an open channel (no secure channel) (Abstract, p.1; §5 PRPKG, p.7)
- Proves unforgeability against Type 1 and Type 2 forgers in ROM under the hyperelliptic curve discrete logarithm problem (Theorems 1–2, pp.9–11)
- Reports total computation cost 1.92 ms (0.96 sign + 0.96 verify), lowest among the four compared schemes (§6.2, pp.13–14)
- Reports communication cost |m| + 2|n| = 1160 bits, lowest among the four compared schemes (§6.3, Table 6, pp.14–15)

## Mathematical Notation

Construction below follows §5, Fig. 3 (pp.6–8); notation verified against the source PDF (no bilinear pairing anywhere).

### Setup (§5 Setup, p.7)

$$
\begin{aligned}
&Hyper \text{ (genus-2 hyperelliptic curve over } F_p), \quad D \text{ (divisor)} \\
&Dot_p \in F_p \text{ (DoT/TA private key)}, \quad Dot_{pb} = Dot_p \cdot D \text{ (public key)} \\
&H_{01}, H_{02}, H_{03} \text{ (hash functions)} \\
&PB_{frm} = \{Hyper, D, H_{01}, H_{02}, H_{03}, F_p, Dot_{pb}\}
\end{aligned}
$$

### Partial Private Key Extract (PRPKG) (§5, p.7)

$$
\begin{aligned}
&G_{usr} \in F_p, \quad K_{usr} = G_{usr} \cdot Dot_{pb}, \quad EID_{usr} = E_{K_{usr}}(ID_{usr}) \\
&F_{usr} = G_{usr} \cdot D, \quad \text{user sends } (EID_{usr}, F_{usr}) \text{ to DoT} \\
&K_{usr} = F_{usr} \cdot Dot_p, \quad H_{1usr} = H_{01}(K_{usr}) \\
&H_{2usr} = H_{02}(EID_{usr}, F_{usr}, Dot_{pb}) \\
&J_{usr} \in F_p, \quad P_{usr} = J_{usr} + Dot_p \cdot H_{2usr}, \quad L_{usr} = J_{usr} \cdot D
\end{aligned}
$$

### Set Private/Public Key (PRKG) (§5, p.7)

$$
\begin{aligned}
&SK_{usr} = (G_{usr}, P_{usr}) \\
&PK_{usr} = (F_{usr}, L_{usr})
\end{aligned}
$$

### Signature (SIGG, by OBU) (§5, pp.7–8)

$$
\begin{aligned}
&T_{OBU} \in F_p, \quad W_{OBU} = T_{OBU} \cdot D \\
&H_{3usr} = H_{03}(EID_{usr}, F_{usr}, L_{usr}, W_{OBU}) \\
&S_{OBU} = T_{OBU} + H_{3usr} \cdot (G_{usr} + P_{usr}), \quad \sigma = (S_{OBU}, W_{OBU})
\end{aligned}
$$

### Verification (SIGV, by RSU) (§5, p.8)

Paper-true equation (SIGV, p.8; Correctness, p.8):

$$
S_{OBU} \cdot D - W_{OBU} \stackrel{?}{=} H_{3usr} \cdot (F_{usr} + L_{usr} + H_{2usr} \cdot Dot_{pb})
$$

Aggregate signature generation and verification follow Han et al. eCLAS [29] (§5, p.8).

## Protocol / Scheme

1. **Setup**: DoT/TA picks the genus-2 hyperelliptic curve, divisor $D$, private key $Dot_p$, publishes $Dot_{pb}$ and params (§5 Setup, p.7)
2. **PRPKG**: User sends encrypted identity plus $F_{usr}$ over the open channel; DoT returns encrypted $(P_{usr}, L_{usr})$ (§5 PRPKG, p.7)
3. **PRKG**: User sets $SK = (G_{usr}, P_{usr})$, $PK = (F_{usr}, L_{usr})$ (§5 PRKG, p.7)
4. **SIGG**: OBU picks $T_{OBU}$, computes $W_{OBU}$, $H_{3usr}$, $S_{OBU}$, sends $(S_{OBU}, W_{OBU})$ to RSU (§5 SIGG, pp.7–8)
5. **SIGV**: RSU checks the divisor equation above; aggregation batches multiple vehicle signatures (§5 SIGV, p.8)

## Security Analysis

- Proven unforgeable against Type 1 (FGR1) and Type 2 (FGR2) forgers in the random oracle model (Theorems 1 and 2, pp.9–11)
- Hardness assumption is the hyperelliptic curve discrete logarithm problem (challenger given $D$, $HDLP = x \cdot D$ must extract $x$) (Theorem 1 proof, p.9)
- Sender anonymity and receiver anonymity via encrypted identities at partial-key request time (Abstract, p.1; §5 PRPKG, p.7)
- No secure channel required for partial private key distribution (PPK property) (§5 PRPKG, p.7)

## Strengths & Limitations

- Strengths: Smallest key size (80-bit HECC) with RSA/ECC-equivalent security level claimed; open-channel key issuance with bilateral anonymity; lowest reported computation and communication cost in its comparison set (p.5, §3, §5 PRPKG p.7, §6.2–6.3 pp.13–14)
- Limitations: Security rests on ROM proofs against Type-I/II forgers only; aggregate phase reuses eCLAS rather than a new construction; evaluation is analytic (MIRACL timings on one desktop CPU), not a VANET deployment measurement (pp.9–11, Theorems 1–2, §5 p.8, §6.2 p.13)
