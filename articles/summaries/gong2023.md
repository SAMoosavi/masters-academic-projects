# PCAS: Cryptanalysis and Improvement of Pairing-Free CLAS for VANETs

## Overview

This paper performs a security analysis of the LICLAS scheme (originally designed for healthcare wireless medical sensor networks) and proves it is vulnerable to forgery attacks. The authors then propose PCAS, an improved CLAS scheme specifically designed for VANET vehicle authentication. PCAS constructs the signature algorithm based on ECC to avoid expensive bilinear pairing and Map-to-Hash operations, and incorporates a pseudonym mechanism for conditional privacy preservation. PCAS supports batch verification, allowing RSUs to verify aggregated signatures from multiple vehicles simultaneously, reducing time overhead. Under the ECDLP assumption, PCAS is proven existentially unforgeable against adaptive chosen message attacks in ROM.

## Relevance to Our Work

PCAS demonstrates the cryptanalysis→improvement pattern that is central to the CLAS-for-VANET field. Its 25% reduction in transmission overhead and 16–25% reduction in computation overhead vs. LICLAS provides a benchmark for our efficiency comparisons.

## Key Contributions

- Identifies forgery vulnerability in LICLAS scheme
- Proposes PCAS with ECC-based pairing-free construction
- Achieves 25% transmission overhead reduction and 16.56–25.34% computation reduction vs. LICLAS
- Supports batch verification for RSU-side efficiency

## Mathematical Notation

### Setup

$$
\begin{aligned}
&s \in \mathbb{Z}_q^*,\quad P_{pub} = sP \\
&D_i = s \cdot H_1(ID_i) \\
&x_i \in \mathbb{Z}_q^*,\quad PK_i = x_iP
\end{aligned}
$$

### Partial Private Key Extract

$$
\begin{aligned}
&D_i = s \cdot H_1(ID_i)
\end{aligned}
$$

### Set Secret Value

$$
\begin{aligned}
&x_i \in \mathbb{Z}_q^* \\
&PK_i = x_iP
\end{aligned}
$$

### Set Private/Public Key

$$
\begin{aligned}
&SK_i = (D_i, x_i) \\
&PK_i = x_iP
\end{aligned}
$$

### Signature

$$
\begin{aligned}
&k_i \in \mathbb{Z}_q^*,\quad R_i = k_iP \\
&h_{1i,j} = H_1(pseu_{i,j}, R_i, P_{pub}) \\
&d_{i,j} = r_i + s_2 \cdot h_{1i,j} \\
&\alpha_{i,j} = H_2(pseu_{i,j}, X_i) \\
&Y_{1i} = (x_i + \alpha_{i,j}) \cdot d_{i,j} + r_i \\
&h_{3i} = H_3(m_i, pseu_{i,j}, Y_{1i}) \\
&h_{4i} = H_4(m_i, pseu_{i,j}, PK_{i,j}, t_i) \\
&w_i = Y_{1i} + h_{4i} \cdot x_i \\
&\sigma_i = (U_i, w_i),\quad U_i = u_i P
\end{aligned}
$$

### Aggregate (CLAS)

$$
\begin{aligned}
&Y = \sum_{i=1}^n h_{4i} Y_{1i},\quad w = \sum_{i=1}^n w_i
\end{aligned}
$$

### Verification

$$
\begin{aligned}
w_i P - h_{4i} Y_{1i} &\stackrel{?}{=} h_{3i} D_{i,j} + h_{1i,j} P_{pub} \\
wP - Y &\stackrel{?}{=} \sum_{i=1}^n (h_{3i} D_{i,j} + h_{1i,j} P_{pub})
\end{aligned}
$$

## Protocol / Scheme

1. **Setup**: KGC generates system parameters
2. **KeyGen**: Vehicle receives $D_i$ from KGC, chooses $x_i$, computes $PK_i$
3. **Sign**: For message $m_i$: pick $k_i$, compute $R_i = k_iP$, $h_i = H(ID_i \| m_i \| R_i)$, $\sigma_i = k_i + h_i(x_i + D_i) \pmod q$
4. **Aggregate**: Aggregator sums all $\sigma_i$ and $R_i$
5. **Verify**: RSU checks pairing equation; batch mode aggregates across vehicles

## Security Analysis

- Proven secure against adaptive chosen message attacks (EUF-CMA) in ROM
- Resistance under ECDLP hardness assumption
- Pseudonym mechanism provides conditional privacy

## Strengths & Limitations

- Strengths: Practical cryptanalysis, significant efficiency gains, batch verification support
- Limitations: Only addresses Type-I and Type-II adversaries, not Type-III
