# PCAS: Cryptanalysis and Improvement of Pairing-Free CLAS for VANETs

## Overview

This paper analyses the LICLAS scheme of Liu et al. [18], originally designed for healthcare wireless medical sensor networks (HWMSNs), and proves it cannot resist forgery attacks (p.1–2, Abstract and §1–2). The authors then propose PCAS, an improved CLAS scheme for VANET vehicle authentication built on ECC with no bilinear pairing and no Map-to-Hash/Map-to-point operations, plus a pseudonym mechanism for conditional privacy preservation (p.1, Abstract; p.3, §2; p.16, §8). PCAS supports batch (aggregate) verification so RSUs aggregate vehicle signatures and an AS verifies the aggregate to reduce time overhead, and it is proven existentially unforgeable against adaptive chosen-message attacks in the random oracle model under ECDLP hardness (p.1, Abstract; p.8–9, §5.2–5.3).

Bibliographic verification: Ad Hoc Networks, volume 144 (2023), article 103134, DOI 10.1016/j.adhoc.2023.103134, received 26 June 2022, revised 11 December 2022, accepted 28 February 2023, available online 5 March 2023, Elsevier, authors Ziyan Gong, Tianhan Gao and Nan Guo (Northeastern University) (p.1, header/footer and Article Info).

## Relevance to Our Work

Interpretation: PCAS exemplifies the cryptanalysis-to-improvement pattern central to CLAS-for-VANET research, so its explicit forgery construction against [18] is directly relevant to our audit (p.5, §4.2). Interpretation: its reported gains over Liu et al. [18] — 25% lower transmission overhead for one message and for 2000 messages, and 16.56% / 25.34% lower computation overhead respectively — provide a dated efficiency benchmark for our comparisons (p.1, Abstract; p.13–14, Tables 5/8 and §6.1).

## Key Contributions

- Gives an explicit forgery attack against Liu et al.’s CLAS scheme [18], showing its signing equation can be recombined into a valid forged signature (p.5, §4.2).
- Proposes PCAS, a pairing-free ECC-based CLAS for VANET vehicle authentication with revised key generation and signing plus pseudonyms for conditional privacy (p.2, Contributions; p.7–8, §5.2).
- Proves PCAS existentially unforgeable against adaptive chosen-message attacks in the random oracle model under ECDLP, via Lemmas 1–4 for Type-I/II single and aggregate forgeries plus Type-III/IV analyses (p.9–12, §5.3.3–5.3.4).
- Shows PCAS meets VANET message authentication, integrity, non-repudiation, identity privacy, unlinkability, traceability and replay/impersonation/modification/MITM/malicious-KGC resistance (p.12, §5.4).
- Reports lower overhead than Liu et al. [18] at equal O(n) time complexity: transmission −25% (single and 2000 messages) and computation −16.56% (single) / −25.34% (2000), with Veins simulations agreeing (p.13–15, §6.1–6.2; p.16, §8).

## Mathematical Notation

Setup definitions below follow the paper’s System Initialization params `{q, P, G, P_pub, T_pub, H, H1, H2, H3, H4}` with `T_pub = s1*P` and `P_pub = s2*P` over an ECC group of prime order q (p.7, §5.2).

$$
\begin{aligned}
&P \in \mathbb{G},\quad s_1,s_2\in\mathbb{Z}_q^* \\
&T_{pub}=s_1 P,\quad P_{pub}=s_2 P \\
&H:\mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{G}\rightarrow\{0,1\}^* \\
&H_1:\mathbb{G}\times\{0,1\}^*\times\{0,1\}^*\times\mathbb{G}\times\mathbb{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_q^* \\
&H_2:\mathbb{G}\times\{0,1\}^*\times\{0,1\}^*\times\mathbb{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_q^* \\
&H_3:\{0,1\}^*\times\mathbb{G}\times\{0,1\}^*\times\{0,1\}^*\times\mathbb{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_q^* \\
&H_4:\{0,1\}^*\times\mathbb{G}\times\{0,1\}^*\times\{0,1\}^*\times\mathbb{G}\times\{0,1\}^*\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_q^*
\end{aligned}
$$

Hash domains above are the paper’s stated System Initialization domains; in use the pseudonym tuple is passed as the first argument (p.7, §5.2).

### Partial Private Key Extract

Partial-key generation picks `r_i`, sets `R_i = r_i*P`, hashes the pseudonym and computes the partial secret `d_{i,j} = r_i + s2*h_{1i,j}` (p.8, §5.2).

$$
\begin{aligned}
&r_i\in\mathbb{Z}_q^*,\quad R_i=r_i P \\
&h_{1i,j}=H_1(pseu_{i,j},R_i,P_{pub}) \\
&d_{i,j}=r_i+s_2\cdot h_{1i,j} \\
&psk_{i,j}=(d_{i,j},R_i)
\end{aligned}
$$

### Set Secret Value

Each vehicle picks its own secret `x_i` and publishes `X_i = x_i*P` (p.8, §5.2).

$$
\begin{aligned}
&x_i\in\mathbb{Z}_q^* \\
&X_i=x_i P
\end{aligned}
$$

### Set Private/Public Key

The vehicle binds its secret to the partial key via `alpha_{i,j} = H2(pseu_{i,j}, X_i)` and `D_{i,j} = R_i + alpha_{i,j}*X_i`, keeping `(d_{i,j}, x_i)` secret (p.8, §5.2).

$$
\begin{aligned}
&SK_{i,j}=(d_{i,j},x_i) \\
&PK_{i,j}=(D_{i,j},R_i) \text{ where } D_{i,j}=R_i+\alpha_{i,j}X_i
\end{aligned}
$$

### Signature

Signing picks fresh `y_{1i}`, forms `Y_{1i} = y_{1i}*P`, hashes to `h_{3i}, h_{4i}` and computes the scalar `w_i` below with no pairing operator used anywhere (p.8, §5.2).

$$
\begin{aligned}
&y_{1i} \in \mathbb{Z}_q^* \\
&Y_{1i} = y_{1i}P \\
&\alpha_{i,j} = H_2(pseu_{i,j}, X_i) \\
&h_{3i} = H_3(m_i, pseu_{i,j}, Y_{1i}) \\
&h_{4i} = H_4(m_i, pseu_{i,j}, PK_{i,j}, t_i) \\
&w_i = [h_{3i}(d_{i,j} + \alpha_{i,j} \cdot x_i) + y_{1i} \cdot h_{4i}] \mod q \\
&\sigma_i = (Y_{1i}, w_i)
\end{aligned}
$$

### Aggregate (CLAS)

The RSU aggregates by weighting each `Y_{1i}` with its `h_{4i}` and summing the scalars, outputting `sigma = (Y, w)` (p.8, §5.2).

$$
\begin{aligned}
&Y = \sum_{i=1}^n h_{4i} Y_{1i},\quad w = \sum_{i=1}^n w_i
\end{aligned}
$$

### Verification

Single verification checks Eq. (1) and aggregate verification checks Eq. (2) using only ECC scalar multiplication and point addition, with no pairing operator on either side (p.8, §5.2; p.13, Table 3 pairing-free row).

$$
\begin{aligned}
w_i P - h_{4i} Y_{1i} &\stackrel{?}{=} h_{3i} D_{i,j} + h_{1i,j} P_{pub} \\
wP - Y &\stackrel{?}{=} \sum_{i=1}^n (h_{3i} D_{i,j} + h_{1i,j} P_{pub})
\end{aligned}
$$

## Protocol / Scheme

1. **Setup**: TA picks `s1` (`T_pub = s1*P`) and KGC picks `s2` (`P_pub = s2*P`), publishing `params = {q, P, G, P_pub, T_pub, H, H1–H4}` (p.7, §5.2).
2. **KeyGen**: TA issues the pseudonym set, KGC issues `(d_{i,j}, R_i)` verifiable by `d_{i,j}*P = R_i + h_{1i,j}*P_pub`, then the vehicle picks `x_i` and forms `PK_{i,j} = (D_{i,j}, R_i)` (p.7–8, §5.2).
3. **Sign**: for message `m_i` with pseudonym `pseu_{i,j}` and timestamp `t_i`, pick `y_{1i}`, compute `Y_{1i}`, `h_{3i}`, `h_{4i}` and `w_i` as in the Signature box, outputting `sigma_i = (Y_{1i}, w_i)` (p.8, §5.2).
4. **Aggregate**: RSU collects `n` tuples `{pseu, PK, m, t, sigma}`, computes `Y = sum h_{4i}*Y_{1i}` and `w = sum w_i`, and forwards `sigma = (Y, w)` (p.8, §5.2).
5. **Verify**: RSU/AS first checks pseudonym expiry and timestamp validity, then checks the ECC-only equation `w_i*P − h_{4i}*Y_{1i} = h_{3i}*D_{i,j} + h_{1i,j}*P_pub` singly or `w*P − Y = sum(...)` in batch; no pairing check is performed (p.8, §5.2).

## Security Analysis

- Proves single (Theorem 1) and aggregate (Theorem 2) verification correctness by expanding `w_i*P − h_{4i}*Y_{1i}` to `h_{3i}*D_{i,j} + h_{1i,j}*P_pub` (p.8–9, §5.3.1–5.3.2).
- Proves existential unforgeability against adaptive chosen-message attacks in the random oracle model under ECDLP hardness for Type-I adversaries (Lemmas 1–2) and Type-II/malicious-KGC adversaries (Lemmas 3–4) via the forking lemma (p.9–12, §5.3.3).
- Analyses Type-III and Type-IV adversaries by reduction to ECDLP / hash preimage resistance, concluding neither can forge with known `(pseu, PK, m, t, sigma)` material (p.11–12, §5.3.4).
- Shows conditional privacy via TA-only pseudonym mapping `RID = pid xor H(s1*K, T)`, plus unlinkability from fresh `(k, r, y1)` randomness and TA traceability/revocation (p.12, §5.4).
- Claims resistance to replay (timestamps plus verification equations), impersonation, modification, man-in-the-middle and malicious-KGC attacks (p.12, §5.4).

## Performance

Transmission sizes use 160-bit `q`, 320-bit ECC points and 160-bit hashes versus 1024-bit pairing groups at equal security level (p.13, §6.1, Table 3). Computation timings use MIRACL with `T_ecsm = 0.1652 ms` and the Table 7 operation counts (p.13–14, §6.1, Tables 6–7).

| Metric | LICLAS [18] | PCAS | Improvement |
|--------|-------------|------|-------------|
| Transmission (single msg) | `2|G| = 640` bit (p.13, Table 4) | `|G|+\|Zq\| = 480` bit (p.13, Table 4) | 25% reduction `(640−480)/640` (p.13, §6.1; Table 5) |
| Transmission (2000 msgs) | `2|G| = 640` bit fixed aggregate (p.13, Table 4) | `|G|+\|Zq\| = 480` bit fixed aggregate (p.13, Table 4) | 25% reduction `(640−480)/640` (p.13, §6.1; Table 5) |
| Computation (single msg) | sign `0.3358 ms` + verify `0.6704 ms` (p.14, Table 7) | sign `0.1706 ms` + verify `0.6690 ms` (p.14, Table 7) | 16.56% reduction `[(0.3358+0.6704)−(0.1706+0.6690)]/(0.3358+0.6704)` (p.14, §6.1; Table 8) |
| Computation (2000 msgs) | aggregate + verify per Table 7 formulas (p.14, Table 7) | aggregate `0.1684n+0.0014 ms` + verify `0.3368n+0.1652 ms` (p.14, Table 7) | 25.34% reduction per the 2000-message formula ratio (p.14, §6.1; Table 8) |

Time complexity is `O(1)` for sign/verify and `O(n)` for aggregate/aggregate-verify, identical to the compared schemes, so the gains hold at equal asymptotic complexity (p.15, §6.1, Table 9). Veins/OMNeT++/SUMO simulation agrees: single sign/verify faster than [17,18] and 500-message delay about 25% below [18] (p.14–15, §6.2).

## Strengths & Limitations

- Strength: gives a checkable forgery derivation for [18] rather than asserting insecurity, pinpointing the `W_i` design flaw (p.5–6, §4.2).
- Strength: removes both pairings and Map-to-point hashes while keeping fixed-size aggregates (`480` bit) and ECC-only verification (p.13, Tables 3–4; p.16, §8).
- Limitation: authors state pseudonym generation is too complicated with too many entity interactions, leaving efficient/self-computed pseudonyms to future work (p.16, §7).
- Limitation: authors note failed aggregate verification forces re-verification with large overhead, plus fixed rather than dynamic batch sizes and expired/unverified BSM buildup under dense traffic (p.16, §7).
